# Q2-P1-E01 — Quality: what the frozen frontend/scorer can reach before CPU constraints

Generated by `edgelab.analysis.q2_p1_e01` (git `f87ac792`); pre-registered in `plan.md`, locked in `config.yaml`; per-video numbers in `results.csv`, every decision in `decisions.jsonl`, all aggregates in `summary.json`. No training was done: every model is an existing checkpoint / detector cache (E00 locked splits, hashes and causal S3/S4 protocol). Scores are frame-level AUC x 100 pooled over the clips of the stated split; bootstrap CIs resample *videos* (1000 resamples, seed 0, paired where it is a difference).

## 1. Answer

**1. Offline ceiling vs the deployable causal protocol (ShanghaiTech).** With the paper's offline protocol (S0) the released AlphaPose poses reproduce 85.94; the best frozen detector (ML0 @640, NCNN FP16) reaches 78.00 and MLP 79.24, against 68.53 for stock YOLOv8n-pose @640 and 70.70 @800. Under the causal protocol that E02 must beat (S4) the numbers are released 80.85, ML0 73.78 (dev 72.39 / confirmation 74.32), MLP 74.69, stock@640 66.03, stock@800 67.51, stock@960 68.09. Causality costs 4.2 pt for ML0; the gap to the oracle poses is 7.1 pt (S4) / 7.9 pt (S0) and comes from the frontend (pose quality and tracking, items 5-6): the scorer is identical.

**2. Existing ML0 vs stock (ShanghaiTech, S4 all, paired video bootstrap).** ML0@640 − stock@640 = +7.75 [+4.63, +10.47]\* (dev +5.89 [+1.01, +10.31]\*, confirmation +8.84 [+4.52, +12.89]\*); ML0@640 − stock@800 at equal runtime (both NCNN FP16) = +6.31 [+3.23, +9.17]\* (dev +4.78 [-0.37, +9.28], confirmation +7.24 [+2.80, +11.23]\*). The partial-label recipe therefore beats *more resolution* by a margin larger than stock@480→960 buys (+2.07 [+0.74, +3.57]\* for 640→960), and ML0 − M00 = +11.66 [+8.25, +14.82]\* shows the gain comes from the labels, not from the extra training epochs (M00 is *worse* than stock: -4.11 [-6.54, -1.77]\*). NCNN FP16 vs MPS FP32 is lossless: ML0 +0.20 [-0.04, +0.48], MLP -0.14 [-0.41, +0.10], stock@800 -0.04 [-0.20, +0.11].

**3. The P2 head (MLP − ML0) is small and not confirmed.** NCNN: +0.91 [-0.02, +1.81] (dev +1.96 [+0.31, +3.64]\*, confirmation +0.30 [-0.67, +1.28]); MPS: +1.25 [+0.36, +2.14]\* (dev +2.14 [+0.43, +3.73]\*, confirmation +0.73 [-0.27, +1.77]) for 1.92x the FLOPs. Frontend lock (F1-F3, dev data only): **`ML0_ncnn`** — MLP misses the pre-registered +2.0 pt dev margin (dev gain +1.96, a shortfall of 0.04 pt although its dev CI excludes 0) and its advantage vanishes on held-out clips and under the locked tracker (SHT S4 all with the locked tracker: MLP 76.55 vs ML0 76.82; point estimates, not a pre-registered test). The P2 head's recall gain is limited to the 48-96 px bins (+1.2 [+0.8, +1.6] pt).

**4. UBnormal does not show the ML0 advantage.** ML0 − stock@640 = +0.32 [-3.43, +3.69] (dev -0.79 [-7.91, +5.07], confirmation +0.98 [-2.98, +5.26]), ML0 − stock@960 = -3.52 [-6.93, -0.43]\*; MLP − ML0 = -1.04 [-3.40, +1.15]. UBnormal S4 AUCs are released 71.58, stock 57.72, ML0 58.04, stock@960 61.56 (S0: 71.78 / 55.76 / 54.37 / 59.01). The ShanghaiTech advantage of the partial-label recipe does not transfer to UBnormal, so the quality lock rests on ShanghaiTech and UBnormal is used as a no-harm check (MPS proxies: no NCNN cache exists).

**5. Small people.** Recall against the released-AlphaPose reference (ShanghaiTech, 187,021 person-frames): ML0 17.2% / 54.1% / 81.2% / 88.6% / 96.1% for <32 / 32-48 / 48-64 / 64-96 / 96-160 px (≥160: 98.5%), stock@640 0.1% / 0.7% / 5.7% / 61.3% / 96.3%; overall 72.9% vs 39.6%. ML0 fixes the mid-size people (48-96 px) and half of the 32-48 px ones but still finds only 17.2% of the <32 px people; stock@800 and the P2 head move the small-person recall by only +0.3 pt and -0.1 pt in the 32-48 px bin. 84.2% of ML0's 50,657 missed person-frames are *intermittent* (people it sees in other frames of the same track; stock: 49.5%), and 52.3% of the ML0 misses have a raw-cache box with 0.05 ≤ conf < 0.25 (48.5% inside intermittent tracks where a two-stage association could use it, 3.8% in never-seen tracks).

**6. Tracking gaps and stranded windows.** ML0 produces 2,006 tracks on ShanghaiTech (released 1,415, stock 880); 48% of them are shorter than 24 poses (released 26%, stock 38%) and hold 6.3% of the poses; 1,350 within-track gaps are ≥3 frames (released 1,946) and 8% of the poses sit in post-gap warm-up. Net: 80.8% of frames receive a fresh window score with ML0 (released 86.1%, stock 59.7%; anomalous frames 91.4% vs 67.8%). The ML0 detector's extra recall comes with more track fragmentation, which is the lever the tracker sweep tests.

Mechanism (factor marginals, section 6): the two-stage low threshold carries the effect (dev mean over cells: disabled 72.86, 0.10 75.11, 0.05 76.22); the lock also lowers c to 0.20 and raises match_thresh to 0.85; track_buffer (30 vs 45) is irrelevant (74.73 vs 74.73). Tracks drop from 2,006 to 1,766, scored-frame coverage rises 80.8% → 84.4%. The frontend ordering is unchanged under the locked tracker (SHT S4 all: ML0 76.82, MLP 76.55, stock@640 67.12).

### 4.1 Miss decomposition (EXP-022): never-seen vs intermittent, and what the raw cache holds

A reference track is *never seen* if no frame is matched, *intermittent* if some frames are. Each missed person-frame is also classed by the confidence of the best raw-cache box that overlaps it (IoU ≥ 0.3, conf ≥ 0.01): boxes with 0.05 ≤ conf < 0.25 exist in the cache but are removed by the 0.25 gate. ## 9. Limitations and honest caveats

* **Dev is small.** ShanghaiTech has 35 dev clips (10 scenes); dev CIs are wide (about ±4 pt on a single series) and the dev-only choices (frontend lock, tracker sweep) can only separate effects of several points. That is why the rules require a margin *and* a CI, and why the confirmation and full-set numbers are always shown next to the dev ones.
* **Single seed, fixed checkpoints.** No model was retrained; every number is one deterministic replay of a cached detector (seed 0 only fixes the bootstrap). Differences between MPS FP32 and NCNN FP16 detections are tiny (section 3.1) but caches were produced once.
* **UBnormal has no NCNN, no @800 and no ML0/MLP NCNN cache**; its quality claims use MPS FP32 proxies and stock@960 as the high-resolution comparator. Its stranded-window analysis for NCNN arms is therefore absent.
* **Reference for recall is a model, not ground truth.** Recall is measured against released AlphaPose person boxes (EXP-011/022 protocol); a person the reference misses cannot be a miss here, and a box that matches a reference box is not necessarily a *good pose*. Pose quality is reflected only through the scorer.
* **"Recoverable" is an upper bound.** A 0.05-0.25 cached box that overlaps a missed person (IoU ≥ 0.3) may be a duplicate, a part, or a false positive; the sweep, not the count, decides whether it helps. Second-stage boxes here may only continue live tracks (BoT-SORT semantics); spawning tracks from low-confidence boxes is the `c` factor of the grid.
* **Tracker settings are replayed from the detector cache** (conf ≥ 0.01, NMS IoU 0.7 fixed at detection time): any setting that would change NMS or input resolution is a different frontend, not a tracker cell. GMC and ReID stay disabled (as in E00).
* **Wilson intervals (QB2) treat person-frames as independent**, which they are not (the same person appears in hundreds of frames), so they are far too narrow; the paired video-bootstrap recall differences in section 4 are the honest uncertainty, and they also exclude 0 for ML0 vs stock in the <48 px and 48-96 px groups.
* **Selection bias of the tracker lock.** The cell was chosen as the best of 36 on 35 dev clips; the dev gain (+5.0) shrank to +2.0 on confirmation (CI lower bound +0.2). The confirmation-set effect is the number to plan with; the confirmation clips informed only T3 (a revert-only check), not the choice.
* **The frontend lock was decided at the E00 default tracker** (pre-registered order: frontend first, tracker second). Section 6.1 shows the frontend ordering under the locked tracker as point estimates only; no re-selection was done.
* **No cost claims.** FLOPs ratios are nominal; wall-clock, memory and CPU feasibility are E02's question, not measured here.
* **The scorer is frozen** (trained on the released AlphaPose poses); E01 does not separate how much of the released-vs-detector gap is missed people, tracking fragmentation or a pose-domain shift of the scorer's input. Retraining the scorer is out of scope.
