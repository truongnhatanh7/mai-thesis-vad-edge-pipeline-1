## Observations
- GAU is **not better** on the reference poses (−0.78 paper, −0.15 live, both CIs include 0) and **indistinguishable** on edge and top-down poses. All differences are inside the video-bootstrap noise (±1.4 AUC) and the seed spread (the top-down seed differences range from −1.75 to +1.42).
- GAU fits the training set much better: final-epoch mean training loss −0.34 / −0.22 / −0.35 bits/dim (3 seeds) against 0.89 / 0.94 / 0.91 for the standard model, but this does not transfer to test AUC. Training cost 165 min vs 48–57 min (CPU, 2 threads, shared machine). This is consistent with over-fitting the training poses, but I did not test that explanation (no regularisation or early-stopping study; the recipe was frozen).
- Cost: 9.4× the parameters, 2.06× the CPU time per window; G3 passes by 0.07 ms on this Mac, and a Pi-class CPU would be slower (not measured).
- Sanity check: our retrained standard models (85.00 / 85.22 / 85.08; mean 85.10) are within 0.9 of the released checkpoint (85.94, reproduced exactly by our scoring path) and of the EXP-001 control (85.31).

## Deviations and caveats
- Training loop is our own re-implementation of the EXP-001 recipe (`edgelab.pose.stgnf_train`), so the standard seeds are not bit-identical to EXP-001; 973 of 145,162 windows (0.67%) are skipped in all six trainings (Amendment 1 note).
- Trained on CPU with 2 threads each, 4 jobs in parallel with other lanes' jobs; this affects wall-clock only.
- GAU configuration was fixed before any result and never tuned (one configuration, one recipe). A different configuration or regularisation might behave differently; this experiment says nothing about those.
- G3 was measured on the Mac (1 torch thread), not on a Pi or the VM.
