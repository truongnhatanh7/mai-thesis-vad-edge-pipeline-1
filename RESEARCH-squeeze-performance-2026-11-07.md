## 0. What our own diagnosis implies for the search (read this first)

Measured (from RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS / EXP-009): reference (AlphaPose) poses 85.9 AUC, our edge front end 68.5; with reference-quality poses restricted to the people the edge detector finds: 69.2, so **96% of the gap is "which people/frames are detected"**, ~0.7 pt is pose quality. Thresholds, YOLOv8s, STG-NF retraining do not help; resolution helps offline (+3.2 / +2.0 SHT, +3.3 / +2.9 UB for 640 -> 960) but its CPU cost drops frames under a 24 fps deadline.

Three facts I found/derived that shape everything below:

1. **Frames are 856x480 and the pipeline already uses rectangular letterbox** (`ort_pose.py` docstring: R=480 -> 288x480, 640 -> 384x640, 800 -> 480x800, 960 -> 544x960). Pixel counts **[derived]**: 640 = 245,760 px (1.00x), 800 = 384,000 (1.56x), 960 = 522,240 (2.13x). Compute of a CNN scales roughly with pixels, so "buying" 800 px costs 1.56x and 960 px costs 2.13x of today's detector. This is the exchange rate any compression trick has to beat. Because frames are already small, tiling (SAHI) cannot add information: it is just a different way of raising resolution (see D).
2. **A Raspberry Pi 5 is deep in the "slow device" regime, not at s = 1.** Community benchmark of YOLOv8n *detection* at 640x640 square on a Pi 5: NCNN 137.9 ms, ONNX Runtime 219.9 ms, PyTorch 314.1 ms (source in B). Scaling by our rect/square pixel ratio 0.6 **[derived, assumes linear scaling]** gives ~80-130 ms for the detector alone, i.e. s ~ 2-4 against a 41.7 ms/frame budget, before tracking and STG-NF. That is exactly the s = 2 / s = 4 rows of EXP-009 where P-fill gains the most (+2.8 / +5.6 SHT). So on the target device, **filling dropped frames is not a nicety, it is the operating point**, and every speed trick below should be priced as "how much does it reduce s".
3. **A plausible mechanism for the recall cliff (needs a free check, see Exp 1).** The COCO keypoint evaluation page states "Small objects (segment area < 32^2) do not contain keypoint annotations", and COCO-WholeBody states small objects have `num_keypoints = 0`. A ~50 px-tall person in a 856-wide frame is ~37 px tall after the 0.75 letterbox scale, i.e. area well below 32^2 = 1024 px^2 **[derived: ~37 x ~15 px]**. **[inference]** A COCO-pose-trained YOLOv8n has therefore seen very little keypoint supervision at the scale of our far-field people, which would explain (a) the recall cliff, (b) why upscaling helps (it moves people into the annotated size range), and (c) why threshold changes cannot help (no signal to threshold). This is a hypothesis, not a finding; I did not verify how Ultralytics' COCO-pose converter treats zero-keypoint persons. A recall-vs-person-height curve computed from existing cached detections vs the released AlphaPose poses would confirm or kill it at zero compute cost.

Also relevant to ranking: the dominant gap is **not** low light (clean L0 gap is 17 AUC), so low-light-specific tools (TENT, weight-space low-light robustness) address a second-order problem for this thesis.

## A. Weight-space methods

**Plain-language explanation.** A trained network is one point in a huge space of weight values. Fine-tuned copies of the *same* pretrained network usually stay in one "valley" of the loss landscape, so you can average their weights (or move part-way between two of them) and get a single new network of the *same size and speed* that often works better than any parent. It costs training time and nothing at inference. Variants differ in what they average: checkpoints along one training run (SWA/EMA), many fine-tunes with different hyper-parameters (soups), the pretrained and the fine-tuned model (WiSE-FT), or "difference vectors" of several specialised models (task arithmetic, TIES, DARE, Fisher). An *output* ensemble instead runs k models and averages predictions, so it costs k x inference, which an edge CPU cannot afford.

### Key sources

- Model soups: average weights of many fine-tunes of one pretrained model -> one model with no extra inference/memory; greedy soup (add a member only if held-out accuracy does not drop) beats uniform; ViT-G soup 90.94% ImageNet top-1; relates weight averaging to logit ensembling via loss flatness and prediction confidence -> ICML 2022; https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482 ; read: full (abstract + intro + method passages). Note: validated on classification/NLP with large transformers; I found no detection/pose-detector soup result.
- WiSE-FT: linearly interpolate zero-shot and fine-tuned weights, theta = (1-alpha) theta0 + alpha theta1 -> under distribution shift +4 to +6 pp over prior work on ImageNet shifts while keeping/raising in-distribution accuracy; "no additional computational cost during fine-tuning or inference" -> CVPR 2022; https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01903 , https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/html/Wortsman_Robust_Fine-Tuning_of_Zero-Shot_Models_CVPR_2022_paper.html ; read: full (abstract + method).
- WiSE-OD: the detection version, ensembling RGB-pretrained (zero-shot) and infrared-fine-tuned detector weights -> improves cross-modality and corruption robustness on four RGB-pretrained detectors, "without any additional training or inference costs"; benchmarks LLVIP-C / FLIR-C (low-light/night use case) -> WACV 2026; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Medeiros_WiSE-OD_Benchmarking_Robustness_in_Infrared_Object_Detection_WACV_2026_paper.html ; read: abstract (full abstract page). Closest published precedent for weight-space ensembling of a detector under shift.
- SWA for detection: continue training 12 extra epochs with cyclical LR, average the 12 checkpoints -> ~+1.0 COCO AP, no inference cost, across Mask/Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, FCOS, YOLOv3, VFNet -> technical report (arXiv 2012.12645, 2020); https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12645 ; read: full (short report). Original SWA: Izmailov et al., flatter optima, "almost no computational overhead" -> UAI 2018 (page layout text says UAI); https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05407 ; read: abstract + intro.
- Task arithmetic: task vector = fine-tuned minus pretrained weights; add/negate vectors to edit behaviour, no data from the target task needed for analogies -> (venue from memory: ICLR 2023); https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089 ; read: abstract. TIES-Merging: trim small updates, elect a sign per parameter, merge only sign-agreeing ones to reduce interference -> (venue from memory: NeurIPS 2023); https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01708 ; read: abstract. DARE: randomly drop a fraction p of delta parameters and rescale by 1/(1-p); 90-99% can be dropped "more pronounced in large-scale LMs" (larger models tolerate larger p) -> ICML 2024; https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03099 , https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/yu24p.html ; read: full for method + the larger-models-tolerate-larger-p statement; language models only. Fisher-weighted averaging: weight each parameter by its diagonal Fisher information (Laplace view of merging); better than plain averaging for robust fine-tuning and ensembling -> NeurIPS 2022; https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/70c26937fbf3d4600b69a129031b66ec-Paper-Conference.pdf ; read: abstract + method.
- Merging detectors: DuET applies task-arithmetic merging to detectors (YOLO11, RT-DETR) for class + domain incremental learning, +11.39% retention-adaptability index on a Diverse Weather series with 88.57% average retention -> ICCV 2025; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Monga_DuET_Dual_Incremental_Object_Detection_via_Exemplar-Free_Task_Arithmetic_ICCV_2025_paper.html ; read: abstract. Evidence that merging works for YOLO-class detectors for domain shifts, not for recall of tiny objects.
- Weight vs output ensembling: DiWA shows weight averaging approximates ensembling when models are accurate, diverse and linearly connected, and that WA gains are largest when the *input marginal* shifts (variance term) -> NeurIPS 2022; https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/46108d807b50ad4144eb353b5d0e8851-Abstract-Conference.html ; read: abstract + intro. Lookaround: average networks trained from a common point on different augmentations at every step -> NeurIPS 2023; https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/5b4b967d4222d87fa5b28b6ec7144058-Paper-Conference.pdf ; read: abstract + intro (CIFAR/ImageNet classification).
- LoRA / adapters for YOLO: LoRAE (low-rank adapters for CNNs, ~4% trainable parameters, fusable into the backbone so graph and MACs are unchanged; YOLOv8x detection experiments) -> PMC-hosted journal article (journal/year not confirmed); https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12475476/ ; read: abstract + method excerpt. YOLO-PEFT: structure-aware LoRA placement for YOLO11/12 reaches 0.7138 / 0.7307 mAP50-95 vs 0.6428 / 0.6662 full fine-tuning on VOC07 (preprint, arXiv 2608.07051); https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07051 ; read: abstract. LibreYOLO docs: adapters merged into dense weights at export (`merge_lora_adapters`), so no runtime dependency; https://www.libreyolo.com/docs/train/lora ; read: card.
- Practical caution: an Ultralytics maintainer thread on averaging independently trained YOLO weights says it needs identical architecture and training lineage and must be validated; a contributor says independent solutions "would more likely than not lead to a worse model" -> https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/issues/14084 ; read: full thread excerpt (not peer reviewed). Consistent with soups requiring a common pretrained start.

### Assessment for our diagnosis

| Method | Attacks recall/coverage? | Attacks speed? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| SWA/EMA/uniform soup of fine-tunes | Marginally (generic ~+1 AP) | No | Polish, only after a fine-tune that improves small-person recall exists |
| WiSE-FT / WiSE-OD interpolation (COCO-pose <-> surveillance fine-tune) | Indirectly: protects robustness/low-light while keeping the fine-tune's gain | No | **Most relevant weight-space tool**; alpha can be picked label-free using the coverage proxy (rho 0.87-0.92 out of sample, EXP-005 F3) |
| Task arithmetic / TIES / DARE / Fisher (several specialists: small-person, dark, per-scene) | Only if each specialist already improves coverage | No | Interesting for a "specialists merged for free" story, but evidence is LLM/classification scale; DARE's own result says small models tolerate little dropping (YOLOv8n has 3.3M parameters) -> prefer plain averaging/WiSE-FT |
| LoRA / per-scene adapters | Same as fine-tuning, with a built-in forgetting limit; can be merged at export | No (no speed gain for 3.3M-parameter model) | Optional regulariser and a clean per-camera deployment story; not needed for compute reasons |
| Weight-space vs output ensembling | Output ensemble of 2 detectors = 2x CPU, unaffordable on Pi | n/a | Weight averaging is the only ensembling that fits the budget |

**Could weight-space raise small-person recall or low-light robustness at zero inference cost?** Low light: plausibly yes in the WiSE-OD / DiWA sense (low light is an input-marginal shift, which is exactly where WA helps most), but low light is not our dominant gap. Small-person recall: **only indirectly**, as the wrapper around a fine-tune that actually teaches the detector about small people (family C). Averaging copies that never saw labelled tiny people cannot create that knowledge **[inference]**. Honest note in section 5.

## B. Compression for speed ("quantize to buy resolution")

**Plain-language explanation.** Quantization stores weights/activations as 8-bit integers instead of 32-bit floats, so the CPU can do more multiply-adds per instruction (on ARM, via "dot-product" instructions). Pruning deletes channels; distillation trains a smaller student to imitate a bigger teacher. The thesis idea is: if INT8 makes the detector, say, 1.6x faster, spend that on 1.56x more pixels (800 instead of 640 px) and keep the same latency but find more people.

### Key sources

- INT8 only pays off on the right ISA: identical ResNet-50 INT8 QDQ model on ONNX Runtime CPU, Raspberry Pi 5 (Cortex-A76, ARM dot-product) 144.95 -> 79.08 ms = 1.83x faster; Jetson A78AE 2.11x faster; x86 without VNNI 1.76x slower; Cortex-A53 1.65x slower -> arXiv 2609.16085 (2026, preprint); https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16085 ; read: full for Table 2. ONNX Runtime docs agree: "Arm-based processors with dot-product instructions can get better performance in general"; static quantization recommended for CNNs; S8S8 with QDQ is the default; per-channel and quantization debugging advice -> https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/performance/model-optimizations/quantization.html ; read: full (docs).
- YOLO-pose INT8 on Pi 5, the closest datapoint: Arm's YOLO11n-pose INT8 (ExecuTorch + XNNPACK/KleidiAI, static PTQ, 640x640, 4x A76): 234.0 ms p50 vs 335.84 ms baseline = 1.44x faster; mAP50-95 46.38 vs 47.37 (-0.99), mAP50 -0.16; the **pose head is kept out of INT8** because its concatenated box/score/keypoint-visibility outputs have incompatible ranges ("a single per-tensor quantizer collapses the visibility channel to a constant"), first conv also kept at higher precision -> Arm model card (not peer reviewed); https://huggingface.co/Arm/yolo11n-pose-int8-xnnpack-executorch-raspberrypi5 ; read: card (full).
- PTQ on the pose head can hurt badly: Espressif's YOLO11n-pose deployment notes: float 50.0 -> 43.9 mAP50:95 after 8-bit PTQ, 44.9 after 8-bit QAT (their ESP-PPQ toolchain, microcontroller target); they split box/score branches and move decoding out of the network to reduce error -> https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dl/en/latest/tutorials/how_to_deploy_yolo11n-pose.html ; read: card. Ultralytics issue #21625 explains why (pose output concatenates quantities with different ranges; maintainers suggest post-editing the graph or OpenVINO INT8) -> https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/issues/21625 ; read: issue text (not peer reviewed).
- Mixed evidence on how much INT8 buys for YOLOv8n-class nets on Pi 5: Ultralytics issue #7445 reports TFLite int8 395.2 vs 403.9 ms (no gain) on a Pi 5 -> https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/issues/7445 ; a license-plate benchmark finds dynamic/INT8 about 20% faster than float in TFLite/XNNPACK at 640 (YOLOv8n INT8 399 ms vs FP32 498 ms) -> https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13517057/ ; read: abstract + results passages. Treat INT8 gain as 1.2x-1.8x depending on runtime/graph, not as a constant.
- **Backend matters as much as INT8** (community benchmark, not peer reviewed): YOLOv8n detection, Pi 5, 640, 1080p video, 899 frames: NCNN 137.9 ms (7.25 FPS), ONNX Runtime 219.9 ms (4.55 FPS), PyTorch 314.1 ms (3.18 FPS) -> https://kadirmertabatay.github.io/rpi-yolo-benchmark/benchmarks/yolov8/yolov8n/ ; read: full table. NCNN vs ORT = 1.59x **[derived]**. A Korean NCNN study sweeps resolution 640/480/416/320 x FP32/FP16/INT8 x NCNN/TFLite on a Pi 5 (YOLOv8n, license plates) but I only read the abstract -> https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003394282 ; read: abstract.
- Structured pruning: DepGraph groups coupled layers so any architecture can be pruned automatically (>2x ResNet-50 speed-up with 0.32% loss); Torch-Pruning lists YOLOv8 as supported but the paper reports no YOLOv8 numbers -> CVPR 2023; https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12900 ; read: abstract + results. An unmerged Ultralytics PR for YOLOv8s reports ~30% lower ONNX latency but mAP50 0.869 -> 0.687 after retraining on COCO128 -> https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/pull/21977 ; read: PR text (weak evidence).
- Distillation into the small student: Ultralytics documents feature distillation from a same-family teacher at the three neck layers (`distill_model`, `dis`) and states only detect is "experimentally verified"; pose runs but accuracy gains are not validated -> https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/knowledge-distillation ; read: card (check that the installed Ultralytics version has it). Pose-specific KD for small HPE nets (orderly dual-teacher, COCO/OCHuman) -> https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10414 ; read: abstract (single-person top-down heatmap networks, not YOLO-pose).

### "Quantize to buy resolution": what the evidence supports

Iso-latency needs **INT8 speed-up >= pixel ratio** **[derived]**: 800 px needs >= 1.56x, 960 px needs >= 2.13x.

| Evidence | Measured INT8/backend speed-up on Pi 5 | Buys |
|---|---|---|
| ORT, ResNet-50, QDQ (arXiv 2609.16085) | 1.83x | 800 yes (not 960) |
| ExecuTorch+XNNPACK, YOLO11n-pose 640 (Arm card) | 1.44x | ~720 px, not 800 |
| TFLite/XNNPACK YOLOv8n/v5nu (PMC13517057) | ~1.2x | ~680 px |
| NCNN vs ORT, FP32, YOLOv8n det (community) | 1.59x (backend swap, no accuracy cost) | 800 yes |
| NCNN + INT8 | not measured by anything I read | unknown |

**Verdict.** INT8 can plausibly buy 640 -> ~800 px but **not** 960 px, and only if the ORT QDQ graph really reaches >= 1.5x on our pose model (the Arm datapoint for a YOLO pose net on the same CPU is 1.44x). A **backend swap (ORT -> NCNN)** may be worth about as much as INT8 with zero accuracy risk, and the two may stack. Both must be measured on our rect shapes on the VM *and* the Pi (the sign and size of the INT8 effect is ISA-determined, arXiv 2609.16085). Unknown and critical: does INT8 specifically hurt **small-person** recall? The only accuracy figures are COCO mAP (-1 pt with FP head), dominated by larger people **[inference]**. Pruning of an already tiny 3.3M-parameter net is the least attractive option (accuracy collapses in the one number found). Distillation is most valuable as the *mechanism of family C*, not as a way to shrink.

**What it attacks:** speed (reduces s), which indirectly buys coverage. It does not improve recall at fixed resolution.

## C. Domain adaptation of the small detector with free pseudo-labels

**Plain-language explanation.** The YOLOv8n-pose we use was trained on COCO photos, where people are large and small people have no keypoint labels. Our deployment sees one fixed camera with small, far people. "Self-training / distillation" lets a stronger offline model (the teacher) label our own surveillance frames, and then we fine-tune the small detector on those labels. At run time the student is exactly the same size and speed, but it has seen the right kind of data. We already have free teacher labels: AlphaPose poses released for ShanghaiTech and UBnormal (train and test), or we can run a big YOLO-pose offline on upscaled frames.

### Key sources

- Self-supervised scene adaptation for fixed cameras: adapt a detector to one scene using pseudo-labels from the detector plus a tracker (to recover missed detections), cross-teaching, artifact-free location-aware mixup and background input -> on Scenes100 average AP gain +3.76 / +4.45 (AP_m-style metrics), vs plain self-training +0.80 / +1.39 and several domain-alignment baselines that *hurt* (STAC -1.26, H2FA -3.10) -> CVPR 2023; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Zhang_Object_Detection_With_Self-Supervised_Scene_Adaptation_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf ; read: full for abstract, method intro and Table 3 (as extracted).
- Efficiency-preserving scene adaptation (same group): a mixture-of-experts head so one detector serves many cameras with the same GFLOPs/latency as the base model; pseudo-labels from the *base model run on a x2 upscaled image* ("upscaling-based self-distillation"), inference at native resolution; reports that upscaling "greatly improves the detection precision of the base model, likely because ... the object is considerably smaller than the dataset on which the base models are trained"; tested with YOLOv8s, Faster R-CNN, DINO -> BMVC 2024 (URL `efficienntSceneAdaptive-BMVC24`); http://www.minhhoai.net/papers/efficienntSceneAdaptive-BMVC24.pdf ; read: full for abstract, Sec 3.3 sentence and Table 2 caption. **This is nearly the exact recipe we want** (high-resolution teacher, low-resolution student, zero extra inference), for boxes rather than poses.
- Cross-resolution / cross-scale distillation for small objects: MSAD aligns feature pyramids between a high-res teacher and a low-res student; low-res models beat conventionally trained low-res ones by 2.1-3.6 mAP -> CVPR 2021; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Qi_Multi-Scale_Aligned_Distillation_for_Low-Resolution_Detection_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf ; read: abstract + intro. ZoomInNet: double-resolution teacher, same-size student, position-aware feature distillation, improves small-object detection on VisDrone -> Remote Sensing 2021; https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/6/1198 ; read: abstract. ScaleKD: scale-aware distillation for small-object detectors -> CVPR 2023; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Zhu_ScaleKD_Distilling_Scale-Aware_Knowledge_in_Small_Object_Detector_CVPR_2023_paper.html ; read: abstract.
- Small-object training tricks: copy-paste of small objects inside the image plus oversampling images with small objects -> +7.1% relative AP for small-object detection (+9.7% instance segmentation) on COCO with Mask R-CNN -> arXiv 1902.07296 (2019); https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07296 ; read: abstract + method. Simple Copy-Paste between images with scale jitter -> CVPR 2021; https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr46437.2021.00294 ; read: abstract + method excerpt (instance segmentation).
- P2 (stride-4) head: Ultralytics `yolov8-p2.yaml` adds a P2/4 detection output; YOLOv8n 8.9 -> YOLOv8n-p2 17.4 GFLOPs in a practitioner write-up (https://y-t-g.github.io/tutorials/yolov8-increase-accuracy/ ; read: full tutorial excerpt); VisDrone mAP50 +2.94 points (to 20.84%) for a YOLOv8-P2 variant (IEEE conference 2026; https://doi.org/10.1109/icgmrs70230.2026.11542306 ; read: abstract); high-resolution P2 head on YOLOv12 raises VisDrone mAP50 0.63 -> 0.69 and TinyPerson 0.40 -> 0.45 (CVPR 2026 Workshop; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/AERO-HPR/html/S_Enhancing_Aerial_Pedestrian_Detection_via_High-Resolution_P2_Feature_Integration_in_CVPRW_2026_paper.html ; read: abstract). **[derived]** P2 roughly doubles detector FLOPs, i.e. it costs about what 905 px costs (sqrt(2) x 640), so it competes with the resolution knob rather than beating it.
- Why higher test resolution helps even without retraining: FixRes shows objects appear smaller at test than at train time under standard augmentation, and testing at higher resolution (plus light BN/classifier fine-tuning) fixes much of it -> NeurIPS 2019; https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06423 ; read: full for the apparent-size analysis (classification). Consistent with our "resolution is the dominant lever" and with the small-person gap hypothesis in section 0.
- Test-time adaptation for low light: Tent minimises prediction entropy while updating normalisation statistics/affine parameters online (venue from memory: ICLR 2021) -> https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10726 ; read: abstract. On a camera-based 3D detector with low-light corruption TENT *lowered* NDS (0.3212 baseline -> 0.2070) and AdaBN collapsed, while a learnable-BN method improved it (0.3469) -> arXiv 2411.18860 (preprint); https://arxiv.org/html/2411.18860 ; read: full for the table. Continual test-time detection adaptation (adaptor modules, ~20 FPS reported) -> CVPR 2024; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yoo_What_How_and_When_Should_Object_Detectors_Update_in_Continually_CVPR_2024_paper.html ; read: abstract.
- Teacher labels for pose students: practitioners' recipe of confidence-filtered teacher keypoints as pseudo-labels, and an Ultralytics-style feature-distillation option (see B); I found **no** paper that fine-tunes a YOLO-pose model on AlphaPose or high-resolution-teacher pseudo-labels for pose-based video anomaly detection (absence in a web-only search).

### Assessment for our diagnosis

- **Pseudo-label fine-tuning (teacher = released AlphaPose poses on the *train* split, or an offline high-resolution/x2 upscaled YOLO-pose, or both) attacks exactly the diagnosed gap, recall of small people, at zero inference cost.** It is the only item in this document that changes *what the detector knows*. Bound on the gain **[inference]**: the offline 960-px gain (+2.0 to +3.3 AUC, EXP-003/006) is what a student that learns to "see at 960 while running at 640" could at best match, plus whatever scene specialisation adds (Scenes100: +3.8-4.7 AP-style gain from self-supervised scene adaptation, different task). A slope from our own data: in EXP-009 P1 a +0.049 gain in anomalous-frame coverage was worth +0.9 AUC (paper), so recovering the detection-side gap needs large coverage gains.
- Label-format gotchas **[inference, check]**: Ultralytics pose labels need a box plus 17 keypoints; AlphaPose gives keypoints (box derivable from keypoint extents plus margin, which is approximate) while an offline YOLOv8x-pose teacher gives native boxes+keypoints. Keep **train-split frames only** for fitting and evaluate on the test split; using released *test* poses for training would be transductive self-training and must be reported as such. ShanghaiTech's train and test splits share the same scenes (dataset design; verify locally), so gains are per-camera specialisation; a UBnormal replication is needed before claiming generality.
- P2 head / cross-scale feature distillation: plausible but price-equivalent to the resolution knob, more engineering, pose-head variant not verified in Ultralytics. Lower priority than plain pseudo-label fine-tuning.
- TENT/BN adaptation: **not recommended** here; the one detector low-light test found it harmful, and low light is not our dominant gap.

## D. Spatial tricks for small people at low compute

**Plain-language explanation.** Instead of shrinking the whole image to fit the detector, slice it into tiles (SAHI) or crop only the regions where people are likely to be, and upscale those. For aerial images much larger than the detector input this finds many more tiny objects.

### Key sources

- SAHI: slice the image into overlapping patches, detect on each, merge with NMS (optionally plus a full-image pass); +6.8 / +5.1 / +5.3 AP for FCOS / VFNet / TOOD on VisDrone/xView, +12.7 / +13.4 / +14.5 with slicing-aided fine-tuning; inference time grows linearly with the number of slices -> arXiv 2202.06934 (2022; venue not on the page, from memory ICIP 2022); https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06934 ; read: full for the method and Table 1 passage.
- Region proposals instead of uniform tiles: ClusDet (cluster proposal + scale estimation net, fewer chips) -> ICCV 2019, https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Yang_Clustered_Object_Detection_in_Aerial_Images_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf ; DMNet (density-map-guided crops) -> CVPRW 2020, https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2020/html/w11/Li_Density_Map_Guided_Object_Detection_in_Aerial_Images_CVPRW_2020_paper.html ; UFPMP-Det (cluster coarse-detector regions and pack them into one mosaic, a single inference) -> AAAI 2022 (OJS download), https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/19986/19745 ; Focus-and-Detect -> arXiv 2203.12976, https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12976 ; read: abstracts (ClusDet also intro). All aerial datasets, GPU.
- Edge-oriented ROI systems: Mondrian (extract ROIs, rescale each to the smallest safe size, pack into canvases; +15.0-19.7% accuracy and 6.65x throughput vs frame-wise inference on 1080p streams, GPU/NPU) -> arXiv 2403.07598 (2024, preprint); https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07598v1 ; read: abstract + intro. Dynamic Tiling (non-overlapping first pass, extra overlapping tiles only where objects are cut) -> arXiv 2309.11069; https://arxiv.org/html/2309.11069v1 ; read: abstract. AROD (adaptive image cropping, YOLOv2-tiny reaches 29.29 FPS) -> Concurrency and Computation 2021; https://doi.org/10.1002/cpe.6307 ; read: abstract. 8K edge detection with a size-aware density estimator on Jetson AGX Orin: mAP 46.9% vs 28.0% for the conventional crop method at comparable compute, ~4 fps -> IEICE Trans. Inf. Syst. 2025; https://doi.org/10.1587/transinf.2025edp7220 ; read: abstract.

### Assessment for our diagnosis

- **For 856x480 frames, uniform tiling cannot beat "just raise imgsz"** **[inference]**: two native-resolution 428x480 tiles cost about as much as one 864-wide pass, and the benefit comes from enlarging people, which is the resolution knob again. SAHI and its descendants pay off when the frame is several times larger than the detector input.
- **Where a spatial trick can win is region selection on static cameras**: process at 640 everywhere, then give a higher-resolution second look only to the strip of the scene where far/small people occur (a per-camera person-location heat-map built from the *train* tracks, free). Cost = ROI area x zoom^2 **[derived]**. Mondrian/UFPMP-Det/ClusDet are the design precedents. The ROI pass can run at a lower frame rate because P-fill covers the in-between frames. Free pre-check: what fraction of the frame holds 95% of person centres per scene? If it is ~30% or less, a 2x-zoom ROI pass costs about 1.2x of a native pass **[derived, speculative]**.
- Risks: crop-border fragments hurt exactly what STG-NF needs (continuous tracks); more code on the Pi hot path.
- **Attacks:** recall (and, with ROI selection, cost). **Does not attack** the rest of the diagnosis.

## E. Temporal tricks beyond P-fill

**Plain-language explanation.** Trackers glue detections across frames. When the detector misses a person for a few frames, the track breaks and STG-NF (which needs 24 frames with <= 2 missing) throws the window away. Better trackers re-link broken tracks; P-fill (already built) instead invents the missing poses causally.

### Key sources (new here; keyframe/pose-propagation work is already in LITCHECK-2026-11-07-pose-propagation.md)

- ByteTrack: after matching high-score boxes, match leftover tracks to *low-score* boxes by motion similarity, recovering occluded people and rejecting background -> consistent IDF1 gains of 1-10 points when plugged into 9 trackers -> ECCV 2022 (venue from memory); https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06864 ; read: abstract + method. Our BoT-SORT in Ultralytics already uses this two-stage association **[inference: BoT-SORT builds on ByteTrack association; check `track_low_thresh` in our botsort.yaml]**, which is consistent with "lowering thresholds does not help".
- OC-SORT: after a track re-appears following a gap, re-update the Kalman filter with *virtual observations* interpolated between the last-seen and re-detected boxes (ORU), plus observation-centric momentum/recovery -> CVPR 2023 (venue from memory); https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14360 ; read: abstract + method; the Ultralytics docs list an `oc_sort` tracker with ORU/OCM/OCR (https://docs.ultralytics.com/reference/trackers/oc_sort ; card; check it exists in the installed version).
- StrongSORT++: AFLink (appearance-free linking of fragmented tracklets from spatio-temporal cues, 1.7 ms/img on MOT17) and GSI (Gaussian-process-smoothed interpolation of gaps up to a set interval, 7.1 ms/img) -> IEEE TMM 2023 (venue from memory); https://arxiv.org/html/2202.13514v2 ; read: abstract + method; GSI code https://github.com/dyhBUPT/StrongSORT/blob/master/GSI.py ; read: code. BoT-SORT itself says it improves only the online part and uses simple linear interpolation for offline evaluation -> https://arxiv.org/html/2206.14651v2 ; read: excerpt.

### Assessment

- Tracker upgrades fix **fragmentation** (broken IDs and short gaps between detections that do exist), not **missed people** (the 96%). P-fill already bridges short gaps inside tracks; AFLink-style linking would add joined fragments across ID switches, and STG-NF's own half-window look-ahead (~12 frames, LITCHECK Q3) leaves room for a bounded look-ahead link **[inference]**. Expected effect small and unquantified; cheap if OC-SORT is already in the installed Ultralytics. Rank low.
- Learned motion priors and keyframe/MV reuse: already covered in LITCHECK-2026-11-07 (and a deliberate keyframe schedule was a measured loss, EXP-009 P3 -1.8 to -2.7). No new source changes that.

## F. Anomaly-scorer side

**Plain-language explanation.** The scorer sees windows of skeletons and gives each a "how unusual" score. Frames with no tracked person get the "normal" score, so every person the front end misses is an anomaly that cannot be caught. Fixes: (i) make the scorer tell which windows are mostly invented (filled) and trust them less; (ii) train it on the kind of messy skeletons the edge front end produces; (iii) add a second cheap scorer for frames where the pose scorer is blind.

### Key sources (additional to LITCHECK Q3, which already covers SeeKer, STEP, SHARDeg, FineTec, RPC)

- Confidence/reliability weighting inside or around a frozen scorer: STEP (sequence-level weighting by the pose estimator's confidence; 90.1% AUROC on UBnormal, "highly competitive" on ShanghaiTech) -> arXiv 2608.19987 (2026, preprint); https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19987 ; read: abstract. Reliability-aware prototype calibration for frozen pose-flow detectors, +0.34 to +4.49 AUROC (mean +2.03) across two frozen backbones and four datasets -> arXiv 2606.20312 (2026, preprint; from LITCHECK, abstract only).
- Training the scorer on realistic artefacts: augmentations simulating low-confidence missing keypoints, frame occlusion (fragmented tracks), left/right swaps and temporal up-sampling artefacts improve robustness on UAV-Human/JHMDB -> WACV Workshops 2024; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024W/RWS/papers/Cormier_Enhancing_Skeleton-Based_Action_Recognition_in_Real-World_Scenarios_Through_Realistic_Data_WACVW_2024_paper.pdf ; read: abstract + intro (action recognition, supervised).
- Fusing a skeleton scorer with a second scorer: STG-NF + Jigsaw-VAD (RGB) summed scores reach 87.72 AUC on ShanghaiTech with AlphaPose poses, motivated by pose methods only seeing human-related anomalies -> arXiv 2309.14622 (preprint); https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.14622 ; read: full for the Table row and fusion description. A lightweight late-fusion plug-in (three skeleton-derived features on top of Jigsaw-VAD): 84.2 -> 86.5 AUC and cross-fold std 1.76 -> 0.53 on ShanghaiTech; attention fusion ~3.6 kFLOPs -> https://tkuir.lib.tku.edu.tw/dspace/handle/987654321/129889 ; read: abstract. The MoPRL authors list failure cases (missed/occluded people, wrong tracks in crowds, object-relative anomalies) -> https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.03649v2.pdf ; read: full for the failure-case passage.

### Assessment

- **Provenance-aware scoring is free in CPU and fits P-fill**: gate or down-weight windows by predicted-frame share and report AUC with and without predicted-dominated windows (also the anomaly-hiding diagnostic LITCHECK asked for). Moderate novelty because it extends RPC/STEP-style weighting to *temporal* provenance.
- **Retraining STG-NF with P/B-fill noise** (Cormier-style) is a sensible follow-up *only if* predicted windows show a distribution shift; retraining on YOLO poses alone already did not help.
- **A cheap second scorer for "blind" frames** (frames with motion but no tracked person) is the only item that attacks the coverage gap on the *scorer* side, but I found no source with a CPU-cheap scorer on ShanghaiTech; the fusion evidence uses heavy RGB models (Jigsaw-VAD needs per-object detection + cubes). Mark as exploratory with no direct evidence **[inference]**.

## 4. Recommended next 3 experiments (cheapest first)

Each is written so the hypothesis and pass rule can be registered before running. Numbers refer to the existing paired-bootstrap-over-videos machinery (paper and live variants both reported; claims restricted to findings that hold in both, as in EXP-003).

### Exp 1 (analysis + timing only, no training): "Where are the missing people, and what does a faster runtime buy?"

- **1a (free, cached detections).** Using released AlphaPose poses as pseudo ground truth on ShanghaiTech train+test, compute edge-detector recall as a function of person height in pixels (and per scene). **H1a:** recall falls below 50% for people < ~40 px tall (at 640-letterboxed scale) and the fraction of AlphaPose person-frames below that height is >= 50% of all frames the edge pipeline misses. **Pass:** both hold in both datasets; if not, the small-person/COCO-annotation hypothesis (section 0, fact 3) is rejected and Exp 2's expected gain is revised downwards. Also compute the per-scene 95%-mass person-location area (feeds the ROI idea).
- **1b (timing, VM first, Pi when available).** Export (i) INT8 QDQ ORT model with the pose output left FP32 and calibration from *train* frames, (ii) NCNN FP32 (and NCNN INT8 if exportable). Measure per-frame latency at the four rect shapes (480/640/800/960) with the existing timing harness. **H1b:** INT8 or NCNN at rect 800 has median per-frame latency <= 1.10x of ORT-FP32 at rect 640 on the same machine, AND offline paper-variant AUC at 800 INT8/NCNN >= FP32-640 AUC + 1.0 with the paired-bootstrap lower bound > 0, AND INT8 vs FP32 at the same resolution loses <= 0.5 AUC. **Pass:** all three; **partial:** latency passes, AUC not (then the knob is worthless regardless of speed). Then run the EXP-004-style delivered-AUC replay at s given by the measured Pi latency, with and without P-fill.
- Cost: hours of CPU (1a), a day of timing (1b). No GPU/training.

### Exp 2 (training, Mac MPS): "Teach the 640-px nano model to see small people (pseudo-label fine-tune)"

- Arms (all fine-tune YOLOv8n-pose from the COCO checkpoint, *train-split frames only*, test untouched): (a) AlphaPose released train poses -> YOLO labels (box from keypoint extents + margin); (b) offline teacher = YOLOv8x-pose (or the same nano) on x2-upscaled frames, confidence-filtered, tracker-completed (BMVC24 / Scenes100 recipe); (c) a+b union; each with and without small-person oversampling + copy-paste. Inference unchanged (640 rect, same ONNX export).
- **H2:** the best arm raises anomalous-frame coverage by >= +0.04 (absolute, paper variant; EXP-009 P1 saw +0.049 for +0.9 AUC) and raises SHT paper AUC over the 68.53 baseline by >= +1.5 with the paired-bootstrap 95% lower bound > 0, with the live variant not worse. **Guard rails (must all hold):** UBnormal-L0 AUC (transfer check, no UB fine-tuning) not lower than baseline by > 0.5; normal-frame coverage does not rise by more than the anomalous-frame coverage (otherwise the model just detects more of everything). **Fail rule:** if no arm gets +1.0 AUC point estimate on SHT, record a negative result (still a valid contribution: "label-level distillation does not close the coverage gap"). Replicate on UBnormal using its released poses only if SHT passes.
- Cost: training a 3.3M-parameter model on the Mac for a few hours per arm; extraction of cached tracks for ~107 test videos per arm (CPU heavy, schedule when the other experiment is idle).

### Exp 3 (weight space, nearly free given Exp 2): "WiSE-FT / soup of the pseudo-label fine-tunes, alpha chosen without labels"

- Candidates: the best Exp 2 checkpoint (theta1), the COCO checkpoint (theta0), WiSE-FT path alpha in {0.25, 0.5, 0.75}, a greedy soup over the Exp 2 arms/seeds (selection by label-free all-frame coverage on held-out *train* videos, the EXP-005 F3 proxy), and optionally SWA over the last epochs. Average state dictionaries before BN fusion; re-estimate BN statistics if the interpolated model looks degraded (my engineering note, untested).
- **H3:** a WiSE-FT interpolation chosen by held-out-train coverage (not test labels) matches or beats the fine-tuned endpoint on SHT-L0 within CI (difference >= -0.3 AUC) AND beats it by >= +1.0 AUC on at least one shifted condition (SHT-L3 dark, or UB-L0 transfer) with the lower CI bound > 0. **Pass rule for the weight-space claim:** both. **Kill rule:** if every alpha < 1 is no better than max(endpoints) on the shifted conditions, report "weight interpolation adds nothing beyond the fine-tune" and drop family A from the paper except as a negative result. A soup that merely ties the best member is also a negative.
- Cost: minutes per candidate to build; evaluation = the same extraction runs as Exp 2 (reduce by evaluating 2-3 alphas).

Sequencing note: Exp 1a decides how much to believe Exp 2; Exp 1b decides whether the Pi story is "INT8/NCNN buys 800 px" or "stay at 640 and rely on P-fill"; Exp 3 is only meaningful if Exp 2 passes. The ROI second pass (#6) and provenance gating (#5) are the next candidates after these.

## 5. Honest note: what weight-space methods can and cannot do here

**Can**
- Combine already-trained models into one network of the same size and speed (soups, SWA/EMA, WiSE-FT, task arithmetic), so they are the only ensembling that fits an edge CPU budget; weight averaging approximates an output ensemble when the models share an initialisation and a loss basin (model soups, DiWA).
- Act as a **safety wrapper around a fine-tune**: WiSE-FT/WiSE-OD show that pulling a fine-tuned model back toward the pretrained one often keeps in-distribution gains and recovers robustness under shift (including a night/IR detector setting), at zero inference cost. For us that means "fine-tune on small-person pseudo-labels, then interpolate so UBnormal/dark behaviour is not sacrificed".
- Give a **new, label-free model-selection rule** (choose alpha or soup members by the coverage proxy that already ranks configurations out of sample), which fits the thesis's "coverage as common currency" thread.
- Support a per-camera story (adapters or fine-tunes per scene, merged at export).

**Cannot**
- **They do not add information.** The diagnosis says 96% of the gap is which people are detected. A weight average or interpolation of models that never saw labelled tiny people cannot start detecting them; it can only trade between what its ingredients know **[inference]**. The lever that adds information is the fine-tune (family C) or more pixels (resolution), with weight-space methods as the *last* polish.
- They do not change speed, so they do nothing for the deadline problem (s ~ 2-4 on a Pi), which is where P-fill and runtime changes (B) act.
- Most of the evidence is classification/LLM-scale (soups, WiSE-FT, TIES, DARE, Fisher). Detection evidence is thin: SWA +1 AP generic (COCO), WiSE-OD for infrared detection, DuET for incremental detection. Nothing I found tests weight-space methods on a *pose* detector, on a 3.3M-parameter YOLOv8n, or on downstream anomaly AUC. Expect effects of the size of generic polish (about +1 AP) on coverage, i.e. well under 1 AUC, unless the ingredients differ substantially.
- Independent runs from different initialisations do not average (Ultralytics issue thread; soups require a shared pretrained start). BatchNorm statistics are an extra risk for CNN detectors versus the LayerNorm models in the soup/WiSE-FT papers **[inference, untested]**.
- Low-light robustness is a legitimate target for WiSE-style interpolation, but in our data low light is a second-order effect (loss <= 1.5 AUC down to luma ~37).

**Bottom line for the thesis narrative.** Weight-space engineering is worth one *small, cleanly pre-registered* experiment (Exp 3) as the finishing step of the distillation pipeline, with an explicit kill rule. It is not the main lever; the evidence-supported levers are, in order: fill dropped frames (done), teach the detector about small people (Exp 2), and make the runtime fast enough to afford more pixels (Exp 1b).
