## Q1. Low light / adverse conditions degrade detection or tracking via confidence thresholds? Condition-adaptive thresholds or score calibration?

- Tracking-in-the-dark benchmark (LMOT: 32 sequences, paired low-light/well-lit videos, RAW) plus LTrack, a network with an adaptive low-pass downsample module and degradation-suppression training -> says low light is noise/feature degradation that hurts both detection and association; in the real night set, tracking after low-light enhancement and RAW detection did "not even better than Base-all" (trained on all lighting). It never studies confidence thresholds, and the fix is retraining, not threshold change. (arXiv 2405.06600, 2024; https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06600; read: full for the enhancement-vs-Base-all statement, rest abstract)
- Adaptive per-frame ByteTrack high/low confidence threshold from the steepest drop in the sorted score curve -> motivated by scene change (sparse vs crowded: MOT16/17 best at 0.5–0.6, MOT20 at 0.3–0.5); matches ByteTrack at a fixed best threshold, no illumination angle. (arXiv 2312.01650, 2023; https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01650; read: snippet+abstract)
- Calibration framework for detectors with new metrics (QGC/SGC/EGCE) plus D-ECE under 5 intensities of synthetic shift; also tests histogram binning and test-time augmentation -> miscalibration grows with shift; post-hoc calibration fitted in-distribution does not fix it. Synthetic corruptions, not night. (arXiv 2309.00464; https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00464; read: snippet)
- Train-time calibration for detection under domain shift (TCD loss + uncertainty-guided self-training) -> detector scores are miscalibrated across domains (e.g. Cityscapes -> foggy), and calibration can be improved. (arXiv 2209.07601, 2022; https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07601; read: snippet+abstract)
- Uncertainty-based "anomalous detections ratio" for RGB and LiDAR detectors on Waymo -> night driving raised the ratio 1.4x and an obscured rainy lens 2.1x as AP fell; uses uncertainty to flag degraded conditions, not to adapt thresholds. (IEEE Access 2023; https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3270447; read: snippet+abstract)
- BoT-SORT tracker paper -> uses detection threshold 0.6 and new-track threshold 0.7 (reproduced setting) tuned for MOT17 daylight; confirms track-start threshold is a separate tunable gate. (arXiv 2206.14651, 2022; https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14651; read: full for the threshold sentence) [Aharon et al.]
- Tracker documentation (not peer reviewed): ByteTrack's second pass matches low-score detections only to existing tracks; a detection below the activation threshold never starts a track; `minimum_consecutive_frames` confirms new tracks. Ultralytics default BoT-SORT: track_high_thresh 0.25, track_low_thresh 0.1, new_track_thresh 0.25. -> the mechanism "low-score person never gets a track" is by design, but only documented in code docs. (https://trackers.roboflow.com/latest/trackers/bytetrack/ ; https://github.com/NirAharon/BoT-SORT/issues/123; read: snippet)
- Low-light pedestrian/CCTV detectors (non-top venues) -> one 2025 paper picks a confidence operating point (F1 peak 0.35) from PR curves; another weights VIS/IR fusion by image brightness and reports a −0.03 to −0.06 brightness-confidence correlation after fusion. Brightness-conditioned weighting exists, brightness-conditioned thresholds do not. (ICUSI 2025 https://doi.org/10.37789/icusi.2025.26; idealrafuna.com preprint https://idealrafuna.com/papers/multispectral-detection-2025.pdf; read: snippet)

**Verdict for novelty claim.** Pieces exist separately: (i) adaptive confidence threshold for ByteTrack (scene-driven, not illumination-driven), (ii) proof that detector confidence is miscalibrated under shift/night, (iii) tracker docs that expose the gate. I found no paper that (a) measures the share of low-light loss caused by confidence gates versus feature quality, or (b) re-calibrates detector/tracker thresholds from an illumination signal as an alternative to enhancement. Claim (a) "mainly through gates" is open but must be shown with your own decomposition (e.g. coverage = fraction of GT person-frames with an assigned track; oracle-threshold ablation). My search was not exhaustive (Google Scholar not used); do one more pass on "night" + "track initialization" before submitting.

## Q2. Skeleton/pose VAD under low light, darkness or pose-estimator degradation?

- Original STG-NF: adds Gaussian noise to keypoints (AUC stays high up to a significant noise scale), contaminates training data with up to 5% anomalies (<3% AUC loss); its failure-case text says a poorly extracted pose may be flagged as anomaly, "more significant in low-quality videos", and UCSD Ped1/Ped2 are skipped because resolution is too low for skeletons -> pose-noise robustness yes, illumination no; no tracker/threshold study. (ICCV 2023; https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10946; read: full for those passages) [Hirschorn & Avidan]
- Analysis of pose-based VAD on ShanghaiTech and CHAD (AUC-ROC/PR, EER; pose vs trajectory) -> mentions lighting/occlusion as pose-estimation difficulties qualitatively; STG-NF drops 4.3% AUC-ROC vs GEPC on CHAD; no illumination experiment. (arXiv 2303.05463, 2023; https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05463; read: snippet)
- Dual-attention normalizing flow for skeleton VAD -> same Gaussian keypoint-noise test: <3% AUC drop below noise scale 1 on STC/UBnormal, ~6% on the HR variants. (arXiv 2406.02976, 2024; https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02976; read: snippet) [DA-Flow]
- Pose-flow scoring with reliability-aware prototype calibration (preprint, arXiv ID suggests June 2026) -> perturbs only test skeletons with four failures at low/mid/high severity: confidence corruption, coordinate jitter, keypoint dropout, tracking switches; keypoint dropout is the failure case; not real illumination, not detector/tracker thresholds. Closest "pose degradation" protocol; not peer-reviewed as far as I could see. (arXiv 2606.20312; https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20312; read: snippet)
- Sequential keypoint density estimator (SeeKer) -> anomaly score weights each keypoint by detector confidence; ShanghaiTech 85.5, UBnormal 77.9 AUROC (per repo table). Confidence is used inside the score, not studied under darkness. (ICCV 2025; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Delic_Sequential_keypoint_density_estimator_an_overlooked_baseline_of_skeleton-based_video_ICCV_2025_paper.html; read: abstract)
- Pose-feature enhancement for anomaly detection and tracking: multi-pose-estimator candidate selection to keep tracks when keypoints are missed -> says STG-NF-style keypoint tracking "often resulted in lost tracking" under occlusion; 86.4 vs 85.9 AUC on ShanghaiTech with the STG-NF pretrained scorer. Direct evidence that tracking coverage affects pose-VAD AUC, but caused by occlusion, not light. (Int. J. Inf. Technol. 2024; https://doi.org/10.1007/s41870-024-02363-2; read: snippet)
- Pi 5 skeleton VAD (YOLOv8n-Pose -> BoT-SORT -> normalizing-flow + multi-scale stGCN): abstract reports 86.2 / 72.2 AUC on ShanghaiTech / UBnormal unsupervised, 82.4 supervised UBnormal. I could not open the full text (IEEE paywall), so I cannot say whether thresholds, FPS or low-light are reported. (IEEE Embedded Syst. Lett. 18(3) 2026; https://doi.org/10.1109/LES.2025.3618635; read: abstract) [Huang et al.]
- Not verified: a project page (STEP) is said by a search summary to show AlphaPose with a different person detector/tracker (SAM3 video instance segmentation) lifting UBnormal AUROC from 90.6 to 93.2. I did not open it; check before use. If true it supports "coverage upstream of the scorer matters". (https://jakubmicorek.github.io/STEP-demo/; read: snippet only)

**Verdict.** No paper found that evaluates a skeleton VAD under real or simulated low light, nor one that varies detector confidence / tracker thresholds and reports AUC. Existing robustness tests are synthetic keypoint noise/dropout. The nearest evidence for your mechanism is indirect (lost tracking hurts AUC in the IJIT paper; dropout is the failure case in the 2026 preprint). Low light plus tracker gates in a pose-VAD pipeline looks open, but only for the sources I could reach. Note STG-NF's own limitation text predicts the failure mode you study, so cite it as motivation.

## Q3. Does enhancement before a (frozen) detector help at night?

- ExDark study with four detectors and classical + generative enhancers -> enhancement alone lowered detection accuracy; gains only when the detector was fine-tuned on brightened data, whichever enhancer. Directly opposes "enhance a frozen detector". (MMAR 2025; https://doi.org/10.1109/MMAR65820.2025.11150856; read: abstract) [Zarzecki & Radlak]
- Empirical study of low-light enhancement on classification and detection (Caltech256-LL, Dark Class, ExDark) -> effect on high-level tasks "marginal or negative"; says enhancement targets human visual quality, not semantic/structural information. Models were trained on low-light or enhanced versions of the data. (arXiv 2409.14461, 2024; https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14461; read: full for conclusion paragraph)
- Night Object Detection dataset (NOD, >7K images, 46K boxes, person/bicycle/car) plus a detector-guided enhancement module and augmentations -> consistent AP gain on NOD and ExDark only when the enhancer is trained under detector guidance; gains depend on camera (Sony vs Nikon). (arXiv 2110.10364, 2021; https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.10364; read: snippet+abstract)
- Zero-shot day-night domain adaptation for detection (Retinex re-decomposition, mutual feature alignment) -> on ExDark table, YOLO-N 62.7 -> 67.3 with KinD and 72.0 with Zero-DCE; MAET 74.0; their method 78.3. Gains are for pipelines trained/tuned with the enhancer; frozen-detector inference is not isolated. (arXiv 2312.01220; https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01220; read: snippet of tables)
- Two-stage enhancement + YOLOv7 on ExDark -> TBEFN + YOLOv7 mAP 0.574 vs 0.49 without enhancement; ZeroDCE++ ~0.564 vs ~0.555 original-data in the cited snippet. YOLOv7 was fine-tuned per dataset, so again trained-on-enhanced. (PeerJ Comput. Sci. 2025; https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj-cs.2799; read: full for the fine-tuning sentence)
- Tracking in the dark (as in Q1): tracking after enhancement not better than training on all lighting in the real night set. (arXiv 2405.06600)
- Not peer reviewed, useful as a sanity check only: a blog benchmark with bootstrap CIs on a YOLO model: CLAHE-LAB +0.003 mAP (inside CI), gamma −0.025, histogram eq −0.096 vs original. (https://luis-avalos1.github.io/Object-Detection-in-Low-Light-Environments/; read: snippet)

**Verdict.** Direction of effect is mixed: with a frozen detector, enhancement is neutral to harmful (MMAR 2025, 2409.14461, blog); with detector fine-tuning or detector-guided enhancement it helps (NOD, DAI-Net, PeerJ). That fits your "enhancement may not beat threshold re-calibration" idea, but no one tests thresholds as the competing zero-compute option, and nothing is tracking- or pose-based except LTrack. Be careful to say frozen YOLOv8n-pose (not fine-tuned) since the positive results use training. Abu Awwad (in PAPERS) is the edge-enhancement counterexample.

## Q4. Frame drops / frame-rate reduction on tracking-based analytics on edge

- Evaluation of eight MOT methods on MOT20 sub-sampled to lower frame rates (25 fps -> 1 fps) -> all trackers lose MOTA; e.g. MOT20-01 MOTA 60.7 (SORT) / 61.6 (ByteTrack) at 25 fps vs 27.4 / 34.8 at 1 fps; ByteTrack keeps its relative position, SORT drops most. With ground-truth detections ByteTrack drops 95.0 -> 54.3 on 01. (GRETSI 2022; https://gretsi.fr/data/colloque/pdf/2022_benmabrouk916.pdf; read: full for Tables 1–2) [author per URL filename: Ben Mabrouk; not verified]
- Streaming perception metric (sAP) that scores detection at each time instant, accounting for frames that go stale while processing -> offline AP 38.0 falls to 6.2 under streaming evaluation; scheduling, asynchronous tracking and forecasting recover to 17.8 (20.3 with infinite compute); a sweet spot exists. Deadline misses become accuracy loss. (ECCV 2020; https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10420; read: abstract+intro) [Li, Wang, Ramanan]
- Frame skipping during detection in a real-time MOT system on KITTI, MOT16/17/20 -> negligible MOTA/HOTA drop with ~80% speed gain at some skip frequencies. Opposite sign to a naive "drops always hurt"; workload-dependent. (Springer chapter 2025; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-96-0692-4_20; read: abstract)
- Hierarchical surrogate-based frame-skip optimisation with ByteTrack + YOLOv8-small -> skip ratio chosen from image features, frame selection from optical flow, MLP predicts accuracy; MOTA 0.5358 on MOT17 at a = 0.15. (KAIST DSpace; https://koasas.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/342417; read: abstract; venue/year not confirmed)
- Per-object IoU decay model for deadline-aware detector control -> shows at 0.2 s temporal mismatch average IoU 0.73 while some objects fall to 0; +39% streaming AP over 71 scenarios on real edge hardware. (ECCV 2026 submission PDF; https://media.eventhosts.cc/Conferences/ECCV2026/pdfs/6427.pdf; read: abstract+intro)
- Fluctuating FPS from cheap-edge scheduling: semi-partitioned rate-monotonic scheduling of SSD detection raised inference 0.5 -> 2 fps and cut FPS deviation by 68%; authors note fps fluctuation affects detection accuracy. (WF-IoT 2021; https://doi.org/10.1109/WF-IoT51360.2021.9595588; read: abstract)

**Verdict.** Frame-rate and deadline effects on tracking are well established, including that tracker ranking changes at low fps. What I did not find is (i) a study that links dropped frames to track-start/confirmation thresholds (the `minimum_consecutive_frames` / new-track gate) or (ii) any study where the downstream output is an anomaly score. Your mechanism claim is a refinement rather than a new phenomenon; cite sAP and the GRETSI study as the baseline facts.

## Q5. Joint input preprocessing + CPU scheduling / thread allocation with accuracy under deadlines

- DualRT: soft real-time framework for dual-stage GPU-CPU video analytics that jointly controls batch size, resolution, frame rate and CPU concurrency per task with multi-agent deep RL -> +13.3% QoS, +24.6% max throughput vs SOTA. Resolution and frame rate are input-side knobs, but no enhancement and a GPU in the loop. (Concurrency Comput.: Pract. Exper. 2025/26; https://doi.org/10.1002/cpe.70174; read: abstract)
- RegenHance: region-based enhancement for edge analytics with a profile-based execution planner allocating resources to enhancement and analytics components -> accuracy +10–19% and 2–3x throughput vs frame-based enhancement on five edge devices (CPU+GPU class). Closest "enhancement + resource planning", but throughput planning, not OS/thread effect under a deadline, and GPU enhancer. (NSDI 2025; https://www.usenix.org/system/files/nsdi25-wang-weijun.pdf; read: abstract+snippet)
- Adaptive offloading and enhancement for low-light analytics: picks an enhancement algorithm per task by class-activation-map similarity and schedules offloading with a genetic algorithm -> up to +20.83% accuracy, +14–72 ms latency; simulation, not CPU thread study. (arXiv 2409.05297, 2024; https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05297; read: abstract+snippet)
- DeepRT: soft real-time scheduler (admission control, EDF batching) for CV on edge GPUs -> latency guarantees for admitted requests; no input enhancement. (https://www.hongpeng-guo.com/publication/deeprt/deeprt.pdf; read: snippet; venue/year not confirmed)
- Already in PAPERS.md and still the nearest: Chen 2025 (delay attack + taskset pinning + JPEG), He 2023/2025 (JEE), Turbo, ApproxDet, LiteReconfig.

**Verdict.** Joint treatment of input transform and resource allocation is common for GPU/offloading systems (DualRT, RegenHance, JEE). A CPU-only Pi-class study with a factorial design of preprocessing x OS/thread settings and an accuracy-under-deadline metric (coverage + AUC) did not turn up. Weak differentiator on its own because Chen 2025 already combines an input transform with core pinning; differentiate by the tracking-coverage mechanism and the anomaly-score outcome.

## Q6. Recent public-safety edge VAD papers in Scientific Reports / Springer journals (calibration of "publishable")

- Lightweight dual-stream (raw frames + optical-flow-guided fusion) future-frame VAD -> Ped2 98.4, Avenue 87.1, ShanghaiTech 75.6 AUC; 128.37 FPS on RTX 4080 Laptop GPU, 10.21M params, 39 MB; says target is GTX 1050-class edge nodes (~24 FPS estimated from G3D score), not IP cameras. Contribution = model + efficiency table, hardware extrapolated. (Scientific Reports 2025; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-30911-8; read: abstract+efficiency passage)
- Edge-cloud framework for tobacco-warehouse safety (YOLOv8-nano + MobileOne-S on the edge, federated self-distillation, cloud Temporal Shift Transformer) -> 98.6% accuracy, 89 ms average latency, 29% faster than cloud-only; self-collected, industrial. (Scientific Reports, published 26 Nov 2025; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-26011-2; read: abstract)
- Energy-efficient YOLO framework for abnormal-behaviour detection in public areas (histogram equalisation, Adam, tracking over time) -> 99.46% accuracy claimed; no edge-device or tracker-failure analysis found in the abstract. Example of an accepted but thin contribution. (Scientific Reports, published 6 Nov 2025; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-22888-1; read: abstract)
- Multi-camera spatiotemporal framework (graph attention, RL camera attention, contrastive memory, neuromorphic encoding) on UCF-Crime/ShanghaiTech/Avenue -> broad list of claimed gains (e.g. 40% better false alarm reduction); shows Sci Rep accepts large, multi-component claims. (Scientific Reports 2025; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-12388-7; read: abstract)
- Salman 2026 embedded violence detection (already in PAPERS.md) is the Sci Rep CPU-only Pi 5 example.
- Springer JRTIP: my search did not find a 2024–2026 JRTIP VAD-on-Pi/Jetson paper (only a 2021 smoke-detection paper on Pi 3 / Jetson Nano, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11554-021-01094-y). Treat as unconfirmed absence; run a dedicated JRTIP search.

**Verdict.** These papers contribute a new model or a framework with headline accuracy and a modest efficiency table. None combine a controlled degradation (low light) with a system-level factor study or a mechanism analysis. A measurement-plus-mechanism paper with a deployable zero-compute method (threshold re-calibration) and rigorous CIs would stand out on rigor; note reviewers there also accept lower-evidence papers, so the bar is novelty framing and clear contribution, not just rigor.

## Novelty risk summary

**Already done**
- Detector confidence is miscalibrated under shift/night (2209.07601, 2309.00464, IEEE Access 2023).
- ByteTrack-style adaptive confidence thresholds exist, scene-driven (2312.01650).
- Enhancement without detector fine-tuning gives neutral/negative results on ExDark (MMAR 2025, 2409.14461, LTrack real-night result).
- Frame-rate reduction degrades MOT; latency is accuracy (GRETSI 2022, sAP ECCV 2020).
- Brightness-gated or content-gated preprocessing and enhancement-plus-resource scheduling exist (Abu Awwad 2024, JEE, RegenHance, DualRT, Kang 2026 in PAPERS.md).
- Pi 5 skeleton VAD pipeline exists (Huang 2026).

**Seems open (for sources reachable here)**
- Illumination-conditioned re-calibration of detector and tracker gates as a zero-compute alternative to enhancement, evaluated with a frozen detector.
- Quantifying how much of low-light loss in pose-VAD is coverage loss (no track -> no score) versus pose quality.
- Linking CPU deadline misses to track-start/confirmation thresholds, with anomaly AUC as outcome.
- Low-light or illumination evaluation of STG-NF-family models at all.

**Closest prior work and how to differentiate**
1. LTrack / LMOT (https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06600): tracking in the dark; differentiate by frozen detector, zero-compute thresholds, and pose-VAD outcome instead of retraining.
2. Adaptive Confidence Threshold for ByteTrack (https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01650): adaptive gate; differentiate by illumination as the controlling variable, a track-start gate, and low-light edge workload.
3. Zarzecki & Radlak MMAR 2025 (https://doi.org/10.1109/MMAR65820.2025.11150856) and 2409.14461 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14461): enhancement does not help frozen detectors; use as support, and show thresholds succeed where enhancement fails.
Also keep in view: Chen 2025 (OS knob + input transform), RPC preprint 2606.20312 (pose-degradation protocol), Huang 2026 (same pipeline; full text not checked).

**Red flags**
- If your own coverage analysis shows poses/keypoint quality (not gates) explain most of the loss, the central hypothesis fails; run the oracle-threshold ablation early.
- Recalibrating thresholds can raise false tracks; STG-NF flags poor poses as anomalies, so AUC may fall even as coverage rises. Report both.
- Huang 2026 full text is unread; if it already reports detector/tracker thresholds or low-light behaviour, novelty on that side shrinks. Obtain the PDF via RMIT library.
- The "enhancement does not help" literature uses mAP; a reviewer may say your claim is only about thresholds in a tracker, so show a non-trivial enhancement baseline (Zero-DCE++ on CPU) with measured cost.
- Search coverage: no Google Scholar / Scopus pass; absence statements are "not found", not "does not exist".
