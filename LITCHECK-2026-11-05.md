## Q1. Is it noise, not low brightness, that drives detection / pose degradation in low light?

- Benchmark of pose estimators under COCO-C-style corruptions (60 models) -> Gaussian noise, impulse noise and motion blur cause the largest drops, brightness the smallest; supports "darkening is cheap, noise is not" for pose, though brightness there is an ImageNet-C style change, not photon-limited darkening. (PoseBench, arXiv 2406.14367v2, 2024; https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14367; read: excerpt of abstract + results paragraph) [Wang et al.? authors not checked]
- Real paired low/well-lit pose dataset (ExLPose, sRGB, extreme dark) -> says scaling dark frames to restore brightness also amplifies noise and "the degree of noise depends on that of darkness"; failure cases are frames where noise dominates and little pixel information remains. Qualitative support, no darkness-only ablation. (CVPR 2023; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Lee_Human_Pose_Estimation_in_Extremely_Low-Light_Conditions_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf; read: full for intro + supplementary failure-case passage) [Lee et al.]
- Low-light synthesis for detector training (unprocess to RAW + physics noise injection) -> on a RAW-dark test set, unprocessing alone +2.8 AP, noise injection +8.1 AP, both +10.6 AP (42.3 vs 44.7 with real data); says noise is "a key difference between normal images and low-light images". Gain is in training, not a test-time darkness-vs-noise decomposition, but the ordering matches ours. (BMVC 2021; https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2021/assets/papers/0085.pdf; read: full for the quoted Table 1 passage) [Hong, Wei et al.]
- Controlled study of detectors (YOLO, Faster R-CNN) over a grid of brightness scale x sensor-noise level using a CMOS noise model (Poisson-type model A, further noise types model B) -> both lower brightness and more noise hurt, degradation grows with noise at every brightness; also reports small noise or dimming can occasionally help detect missed objects. Closest "grid" to ours but on still images and reports no decomposition of the shares. (MDPI journal via PMC10856852, 2024?; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10856852/; read: abstract + conclusion excerpt; venue/year not confirmed) [Rodriguez-Rodriguez et al.]
- Synthetic RAW light-level augmentation for person detection (photon-flux thinning keeping the sensor noise model) -> evaluates AODRaw detector as a function of mean electrons in the box; real and synthetic low-light metrics statistically similar only with noise-aware synthesis; detections vanish below ~3.5 e; mAP falls from 30% to 10% or less between 1000 and 10 e. Most similar physical sweep to ours (person detection, light level as the axis); single-image, driving, no tracker/pose. (arXiv 2605.22455, 2026, preprint; https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22455; read: full for method + ablation passages) [Pais et al.]
- Already in PAPERS: Michaelis 2019 and Liu 2024 (COCO-C with sensor-modelled corruptions). Use them only as the generic "corruption benchmark" citation; they do not separate dark from noisy.

**Verdict.** The ingredient "noise is what really breaks low-light recognition" is widely implied (PoseBench, ExLPose, BMVC 2021 ablation) and a brightness x noise grid exists for still-image detectors. I did not find a test-time ablation on a person/pose pipeline with darkening only vs darkening + physical noise, paired CIs, in video, which is what your Δ(darkness) = +0.33 n.s. vs Δ(noise) = -1.81 provides. Present claim (1) as a quantitative confirmation in a new setting, not as a discovery. Caveat that matters for reviewers: your darkness-only arm is 8-bit with no gain/ISP, so "darkness is free" holds for that model; a camera that applies auto-gain will reintroduce the noise.

## Q2. CLAHE / histogram equalisation / gamma / learned enhancers helping or hurting detection depending on noise

- Real-world corruption benchmark, with CLAHE, NLM, style transfer and adversarial training as robustness add-ons -> CLAHE helps illumination-type corruptions, but CE rises (worse) on some other corruptions; NLM helps spotty-noise corruptions; effects "limited" overall. A sign-dependent-on-corruption report, frozen detectors. (IJCV 2024; https://wangzhijie.me/assets/pubs/ijcv24-benchmark.pdf; read: full for Findings 4.2-4.4 [already in PAPERS for the benchmark, new angle here]) [Liu et al.]
- Multi-stage CLAHE + Wiener pipeline before YOLO under synthetic degradation 0-90% -> pipeline gains grow above ~60% noise (mAP 61.0 baseline vs 77.0 at 90%); i.e. contrast enhancement needs denoising when noise is high. Smoke images with synthetic noise, detector fine-tuned on this data; modest venue. (Int. J. Robotics and Control Systems; https://pubs2.ascee.org/index.php/IJRCS/article/view/2308/0; read: abstract; year not confirmed) [Syafaat]
- Self-guided low-light detection (ICLR 2026, via a third-party paper note) -> on DARK FACE, "enhancement-target only" 72.9 mAP (noise amplified), enhancement + denoising 72.2 (edges blurred), Fourier fusion 76.6 vs 59.7 baseline; says brightening gives "brighter but dirtier" inputs. Trained-with-enhancement result, not frozen detector. (ICLR 2026; https://en.papernotes.org/ICLR2026/object_detection/self-guided_low_light_object_detection_framework/; read: excerpt of a secondary summary, not the paper; verify before citing)
- Zero-DCE trained with a noise prior for detection (noise-aware curve estimation + post-denoise study) -> says enhancement amplifies noise and that noise-handling is the open issue; +3% precision on ExDark with YOLOv5. (ISPACS 2022; https://doi.org/10.1109/ispacs57703.2022.10082804; read: abstract) [authors not checked]
- Fixed-detector pre-processing ablation on ExDark with YOLOv11n (sharpening, gamma brightening, CLAHE, CLAHE+sharpen) -> CLAHE mAP50 0.277 -> 0.290; sharpening and global brightening underperformed. Same ordering as ours (CLAHE > gamma), but ExDark has no controlled noise axis and the model is trained 5 epochs per variant. (journalresearchhs.org PDF, low-tier venue, year not confirmed; https://www.journalresearchhs.org/_files/ugd/ebf144_33f907061fa1445cab72b0c185c6abb4.pdf; read: abstract)
- Embedded vision system that chooses between AHE and CLAHE from measured noise variance and light intensity -> the only noise-conditioned enhancement switch I found, in a robot arm context (92% result claimed, 0.005 s). Weak venue, no modern detector. (ICICT4SD 2021; https://doi.org/10.1109/icict4sd50815.2021.9396953; read: abstract)
- Already in LITCHECK-11-04 (not repeated): 2409.14461, Zarzecki & Radlak MMAR 2025, NOD, DAI-Net, PeerJ ExDark.

**Verdict.** "Enhancement amplifies noise, so it can hurt" is common knowledge in the enhancement literature, and CLAHE-better-than-gamma recurs on ExDark. What I did not find is a clean frozen-detector experiment that crosses {no enhancer, CLAHE, gamma, Zero-DCE++} with {noise on, noise off} at fixed brightness and reports a sign flip with paired CIs. Your L2 / L2-no-noise rows (CLAHE +3.15 vs -1.49) supply that. Frame it as a controlled demonstration rather than a surprising phenomenon, and stress that STG-NF scores via a tracker, so the sign is on pose-VAD AUC not mAP.

## Q3. Input resolution vs small-person recall; resolution x illumination interaction

- Per-frame adaptive input scale for video detection (regressor predicts next frame's scale from current features) -> says lower resolution can give better accuracy as well as speed; ImageNet VID +1.3 mAP at 1.6x speed. Establishes content-dependent best resolution; no illumination/noise axis, GPU. (MLSys 2019; https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02910; read: abstract + method excerpt) [Chin et al. (AdaScale)]
- Detection under mixed degradation (blur, noise, down-sampling) with upscaling/SR -> upscaling raises small-object accuracy but hurts medium/large objects; best scale varies by image. Good support for "pixels help only where there is signal". (arXiv 2208.03062; https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.03062; read: excerpt of intro + Table discussion; venue not confirmed)
- Controlled degradation study for satellite detectors (SNR, MTF, GSD varied; YOLOv5s, YOLOX-S, NanoDet) -> impact depends on mechanism; GSD (resolution) gives the most consistent shift, SNR/MTF effects depend on model and resolution; severe blur + noise combinations lose most. A resolution x SNR interaction report, in a non-person domain. (preprint/venue not confirmed, "Raw Imagery Impacting Your AI: Should You Care?"; https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/raw-imagery-impacting-your-ai-should-you-care; read: abstract)
- Image-quality study on detector mAP as a function of resolution, blur, noise (including fine-tuned vs high-quality-trained models) -> fits detector performance to an image-quality equation with resolution and SNR terms. (J. Electronic Imaging 33(5) 2024; https://doi.org/10.1117/1.jei.33.5.053051; read: abstract)
- Pose-estimator robustness benchmark -> reports input resolution had no noticeable effect on robustness (relative drop under corruption), while pre-training and backbone size did. A relative-drop result across models, not absolute AUC vs imgsz; it nuances claim (4) rather than contradicting it. (PoseBench, arXiv 2406.14367v2; read: excerpt of abstract)
- Resource-adaptive switching among resident YOLOv8 tiers (nano/small/medium at 320/416/640 px) on Pi 5, x86 and Jetson -> KITTI per-tier VRU recall 24.2 / 41.2 / 59.0%; detection-conditioned lock cannot fire on the 76% of VRU frames the nano tier misses. Gives a citable "small-person recall rises with resolution+size on edge CPU" number (confounded with model size). (arXiv 2606.14716, 2026, preprint; https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14716; read: abstract + Pi 5 setup excerpt) [RAMS]
- Physical-sweep person detection (see Q1): authors note small objects approach the spatial resolution limit and that low light shrinks the in-box dynamic range, and that downsampling distorts RAW noise. (arXiv 2605.22455; read: full for the intro paragraph) [Pais et al.]
- Already in PAPERS: Chameleon, AWStream, JCAB, VideoStorm (resolution/frame-rate configuration; accuracy gains hold for daylight analytics).

**Verdict.** Resolution as the dominant accuracy lever for small objects, and that the best resolution depends on content, are established (AdaScale, 2208.03062, RAMS). Interaction with image quality is hinted (satellite SNR/GSD study, 2208.03062) but I found no person/pose study across light levels reporting that extra pixels stop paying off in deep dark. Your L3 result (+0.3 vs +3.2 at L0) plus the monotone 480->960 gain at L0/L2 is therefore a new data point for pose-VAD; note the CIs: L3 resolution deltas are n.s., so call it "stops helping", not "hurts".

## Q4. Per-condition choice of preprocessing and/or resolution under a compute budget on edge CPUs (2024-2026)

- Runtime controller picking resident YOLOv8 tier (resolution + model) from device pressure with thresholds calibrated from idle behaviour; detection-conditioned override -> resolution/model switching on Raspberry Pi 5 under CPU load, but the trigger is system load not lighting, no preprocessing, no tracker/VAD. Closest Pi-5 "knob under budget" paper. (arXiv 2606.14716, 2026, preprint; https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14716; read: abstract + setup excerpt) [RAMS]
- Multi-expert enhancement + expert-selection module that picks the enhancement per image, trained with detection-guided losses -> per-condition choice of enhancer for low-illumination detection, but learned, GPU-class, no compute-budget term, no resolution knob. (arXiv 2605.06084, 2026, preprint; https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06084; read: abstract) [AMIEOD]
- Raspberry Pi 5 turret that maps lux readings to one of three light classes and loads the best RGB/LWIR fusion YOLO model per class -> deterministic light-conditioned model switching on a Pi 5, but inference is on a remote server, hardware sensor input, no CPU cost study. (arXiv 2512.22263, 2025; https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22263; read: abstract + runtime-integration excerpt)
- Per-frame resolution selection plus conditional early exit for video recognition on Jetson Nano -> up to 3.18x faster on UCF101; selection driven by frame content, not light, recognition not detection. (Big Data Mining and Analytics 2024; https://www.sciopen.com/local/article_pdf/10.26599/BDMA.2024.9020093.pdf; read: abstract) [RSEE]
- Camera-side configuration selection by object motion (frame rate, resolution, encoding quality; SPStream/SPStream+) -> reaches bandwidth/accuracy trade-off for vehicle counting and pedestrian detection; bandwidth-driven, daylight. (IEEE Trans. Computers 2025; https://doi.org/10.1109/tc.2025.3603711; read: abstract)
- Bayesian optimisation of resolution + bandwidth for edge video analytics -> online selection without an explicit accuracy model; no light or enhancement. (WCSP 2024; https://doi.org/10.1109/wcsp62071.2024.10827367; read: abstract)
- Already in PAPERS/LITCHECK-11-04 (not repeated): ApproxDet, LiteReconfig, Anole, Chameleon, Kang 2026 gated dehazing, JEE, Abu Awwad 2024, RegenHance, DualRT, 2409.05297.

**Verdict.** I found no paper that selects preprocessing and input resolution jointly from an illumination/noise estimate under a CPU deadline, and none with a pose-based VAD or tracker-coverage outcome. The nearest pieces split the problem: RAMS (resolution tier by device load, Pi 5), AMIEOD / Abu Awwad / JEE (enhancer by condition, GPU or offload), Kang 2026 (gate an expensive front-end on a cheap statistic, Pi 4). Your claim is a combination plus a measured sign-flip table that justifies the policy, so it is an incremental systems contribution; its strength is the empirical basis (why the policy must be condition-aware), not the controller idea. Hold the "first" wording until the EXP-004 CPU numbers exist.

## Q5. Physically based low-light synthesis for evaluating detectors (justify the darkening model)

- Signal-dependent noise model for raw sensor data: Poissonian (photon counting) + Gaussian (stationary) noise, with clipping, and an algorithm to estimate its two parameters from one image -> the standard Poisson-Gaussian reference for K (gain) and sigma_r (read noise). (IEEE TIP 17(10), 2008; https://doi.org/10.1109/tip.2008.2001399; read: abstract + model section excerpt) [Foi et al.]
- Physics-based noise formation model for extreme low-light raw denoising (shot noise, long-tailed read noise via Tukey lambda, row noise, quantisation) with calibration per camera and synthetic-training result -> shows the Poisson-Gaussian model is incomplete for very dark scenes but the shot + read terms remain the core; justifies citing it as the more detailed alternative and noting your simplification. (CVPR 2020, TPAMI 2021; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Wei_A_Physics-Based_Noise_Formation_Model_for_Extreme_Low-Light_Raw_Denoising_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf; https://doi.org/10.1109/tpami.2021.3103114; read: full for the model sections quoted) [Wei et al. (ELD)]
- Repurposing that noise model + RAW "unprocessing" to synthesize dark training images for object detection -> precedent for using ELD-style synthesis for detectors; synthetic-trained detector close to real-trained (42.3 vs 44.7 AP). (BMVC 2021; https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/2021/assets/papers/0085.pdf; read: full for method + Table 1 passage) [Hong, Wei et al.]
- Physics-based photon-flux thinning with sensor noise model for person-detection evaluation as a function of electrons -> direct precedent for evaluating a person detector on synthetic darkening that keeps the noise model, with real-vs-synthetic indistinguishability check; gain adjustment mattered, read-noise and black-level adjustments had very low impact. Closest methodological peer; preprint. (arXiv 2605.22455, 2026; https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22455; read: full for method + ablation) [Pais et al.]
- Detector robustness under a two-model CMOS sensor noise study (Poisson-only vs additional noises), loosely following EMVA 1288 -> precedent for physically based noise models in detector evaluation. (MDPI via PMC10856852; read: excerpt of Section 3; venue/year not confirmed) [Rodriguez-Rodriguez et al.]

**Verdict.** Cite Foi 2008 for the Poisson-Gaussian form, Wei 2020/2021 for the physical stages and calibrated parameters, and Hong 2021 plus Pais 2026 as precedents for using such synthesis to evaluate or train detectors. All of these operate on RAW or inverted-ISP images; your pipeline darkens sRGB-derived frames without ISP/gain and has no Tukey-lambda or row noise, so say explicitly that it is a Poisson-Gaussian approximation (K = 1000 e-, sigma_r = 2 e-) and that real-dark validation (EXP-005 on LLVIP) is the check, as Pais et al. did with real-vs-synthetic comparison. Keep the Pais et al. comparison in related work: it shrinks any novelty claim about "physically modelled synthetic low light for person detection".

## Positioning

**Claim-by-claim**

1. Darkness alone ~0 AUC, noise causes the loss: **known in spirit** (PoseBench 2406.14367, ExLPose CVPR 2023, Hong BMVC 2021 noise ablation, Rodriguez-Rodriguez grid), **new quantitatively** for a pose-VAD pipeline with a paired noise-on/off arm and CIs. Needs a gain/ISP caveat.
2. Loss is coverage, lowering gates does not help: **not found** in this lane (previous LITCHECK also left it open). Treat as novel; supported by your own co-covered vs overall decomposition. Differentiate from STG-NF's and STEP's confidence weighting, which act inside the score, not on coverage.
3. Enhancement effect flips sign with noise and enhancer: **partly known** (enhancement amplifies noise: ICLR 2026 self-guided note, ISPACS 2022; CLAHE helps illumination but hurts other corruptions: Liu IJCV 2024; CLAHE > gamma on ExDark). **Novel**: controlled noise on/off at fixed brightness with a frozen detector and paired CIs.
4. Resolution is the largest lever, stops helping in deep dark: gain from resolution is **known** (AdaScale, 2208.03062, RAMS tier recall); the resolution x light interaction is **only hinted** (satellite SNR/GSD study, 2208.03062). **Novel** for person/pose across light levels. Report L3 deltas as n.s.
5. Condition- and budget-aware configuration on an edge CPU: components **known** (RAMS, AMIEOD, Kang 2026, JEE, Chameleon, ApproxDet); the joint {enhancer, resolution, OS tuning} selection from light/noise under a 24 fps deadline for pose-VAD is **not found**. Treat as incremental until EXP-004 delivers numbers.

**Three closest prior works and how to differentiate**

1. Pais et al., physical-sweep person detection in low light (arXiv 2605.22455; https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22455): physics-based light-level synthesis for person detection with noise-aware validation. Differentiate by video, tracker and pose-VAD outcome, enhancement and resolution knobs, and the explicit darkness-vs-noise decomposition; adopt its real-vs-synthetic check idea for EXP-005.
2. RAMS, resource-adaptive model switching on Raspberry Pi 5 (arXiv 2606.14716; https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14716): resolution/model-tier controller on the same hardware class. Differentiate by the trigger (lighting/noise, not load), preprocessing as a second knob, and the VAD outcome; note its low-recall finding for the nano tier supports your coverage story.
3. Rodriguez-Rodriguez et al., impact of noise and brightness on detectors (PMC10856852; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10856852/): brightness x noise grid with a CMOS model. Differentiate by video/pose/tracking, paired bootstrap, enhancement and resolution remedies. (Also keep PoseBench 2406.14367 and Kang 2026 gated dehazing in the related-work paragraph.)

**Red flags**

- Pais 2026 and Hong 2021 pre-empt "physically modelled synthetic low light for detector evaluation"; do not claim the synthesis as a contribution.
- PoseBench says input resolution has no noticeable effect on robustness; reviewers may cite it against claim (4). Reconcile: it measures relative drops across models, you measure absolute AUC per imgsz at fixed model.
- Rodriguez-Rodriguez reports small noise or dimming can sometimes help detection; do not write "noise always hurts".
- Claim (1) is bound to your sensor model (no gain/ISP/compression); real CCTV may differ. Claim (3) rests on one noise realisation; the seed replicate is a prerequisite.
- Several sources are preprints (2605.22455, 2606.14716, 2605.06084) or secondary summaries (ICLR 2026 paper note); open the originals before citing. PMC10856852 venue/year, PoseBench authors/venue, 2208.03062 venue not confirmed.
