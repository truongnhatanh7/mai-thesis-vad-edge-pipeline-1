# LITCHECK 2026-11-07 (literature lane 3): codec-style I/P/B frames for pose streams

Scope: the owner's idea of running the pose detector only on keyframes (I), predicting skeletons causally on other frames (P) or by interpolation between two detected keyframes (B), to (1) fill track gaps, (2) replace deadline-dropped frames, (3) save compute through a keyframe schedule. Target pipeline: YOLOv8n-pose -> BoT-SORT -> STG-NF (24-frame windows; frames with no tracked person get the "normal" score).
Excludes what PAPERS.md, LITCHECK-2026-11-04 and LITCHECK-2026-11-05 already cover, unless I say "already covered" (used only for a new angle). Baba et al. 2019 is not repeated.

Read-level key (honest): **full** = I retrieved the page/PDF text and checked the specific passage I cite (not read end to end); **abstract** = abstract or search excerpt only; **code** = I read the source file on GitHub. IEEE/paywalled items are abstract only. Numbers are copied from the pages; nothing is estimated. Search was web search only (no Google Scholar/Scopus), so "not found" does not mean "does not exist". Preprints (arXiv IDs 26xx) are flagged; author tags that I could not read on the page are marked.

## Q1. Keyframe / sparse detection with propagation in video analytics

- Deep Feature Flow: runs the heavy recognition network only on sparse key frames and warps the key-frame feature maps to the other frames with optical flow, trained end to end -> 3.7x (segmentation) and 5.0x (detection) faster than per-frame with accuracy "slightly lower"; larger intervals give up to about 10x with "a few percent" loss; using a fixed or off-the-shelf flow loses much more accuracy than the jointly trained flow. Establishes the basic trade-off (compute falls roughly with 1/k, accuracy drifts as the key-frame distance grows). (CVPR 2017; https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07715, https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Zhu_Deep_Feature_Flow_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf; read: full for the speed-up and ablation passages) [Zhu, Xiong, Dai, Yuan, Wei]
- Detect or Track: a small scheduler network compares the current frame with the last keyframe and decides per frame whether to detect or keep tracking -> beats fixed "detect every N-th frame" skipping and flow-based propagation on ImageNet VID, because the right detection rate depends on tracking quality (adaptive keyframes > fixed). (AAAI 2019; https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/4906; read: abstract + method excerpt) [Luo, Xie, Wang, Zeng]
- Glimpse: mobile object recognition that keeps an active frame cache, tracks locally with stale server hints, and sends only "trigger frames" -> triggers when tracking degrades (std of tracked-point displacement above a threshold) or the scene changes (count of pixels that differ); face recognition precision 96.4-99.8%, road signs 75-80% against 0.2-1.9% without its techniques. The two triggers (tracking-quality + scene-change) are the template for deciding when a pose keyframe is needed and when a new person may have entered. (SenSys 2015; https://people.csail.mit.edu/shuodeng/papers/sensys15.pdf; read: full for abstract + trigger passages) [Chen, Ravindranath, Deng, Bahl, Balakrishnan]
- Reducto: on-camera frame filtering with low-level frame-difference features and a per-content threshold map -> filters 51-97% of frames while meeting a query-accuracy target; criticises Glimpse's static pixel threshold because the same difference value means different things for different content/queries. For us: a fixed difference threshold is not safe; the filter must be calibrated against the downstream metric (AUC), not against detection. (SIGCOMM 2020; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3387514.3405874; read: full for abstract + insight passage) [Li, Padmanabhan, Zhao, Wang, Xu, Netravali]
- MARLIN: runs the DNN only for new objects or large appearance change and uses lightweight incremental tracking in between (mobile AR) -> up to 73.3% less energy than running the best DNN every frame, up to 19x better accuracy than an infrequent-DNN baseline; in at least 75% of cases at most 7.36% IoU loss, and in more than 46% of cases it even improves IoU over the continuous DNN. Tracking between detections can match per-frame detection in a large share of cases, and the failure mode is "new/changed objects", not drift alone. (SenSys 2019; https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~jiasi/pub/marlin_sensys19.pdf; read: full for abstract + intro) [Apicharttrisorn, Ran, Chen, Krishnamurthy et al.]
- NoScope: cascades per-video specialised models and cheap difference detectors to skip frames for fixed-camera binary queries -> difference detectors alone give up to 90x speed-up; whole cascade 265-15,500x real time within 1-5% of the reference net. Content-adaptive skipping is very effective when the answer changes slowly, but it answers "is the object present", not "keep a continuous identity track", so only the principle transfers. (PVLDB 2017; https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol10/p1586-kang.pdf; read: abstract + difference-detection section) [Kang et al.; author list not read on the page]
- Towards Streaming Perception: scores detection at every instant so frames that go stale while the detector runs are penalised -> offline AP 38.0 falls to 6.2 under streaming evaluation; scheduling + asynchronous tracking + Kalman forecasting recovers 17.8; the Kalman filter predicts at every output step and corrects only when a detection arrives, with transition and process noise depending on the elapsed time; appendix reports well under 1 ms per Kalman step (hardware not checked). Closest formal precedent for "predict to cover deadline-induced gaps" (boxes, not poses). Already covered in LITCHECK-11-04 for the metric; the Kalman/forecast detail is new here. (ECCV 2020; https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10420, https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/papers/123470460.pdf; read: full for the Kalman passages and the 38.0/6.2/17.8 sentence) [Li, Wang, Ramanan]
- Motion Vector Propagation (MVP): runs a large open-vocabulary detector (OWLv2) only on fixed-interval keyframes and propagates boxes with codec motion vectors (3x3 grid aggregation) -> ILSVRC2015-VID mAP@0.5 0.609; at loose IoU 0.2/0.3 it is close to per-frame OWLv2 (0.747/0.721 vs 0.784/0.780), and it beats MOSSE/KCF/CSRT propagation at mAP@0.5 under the same keyframe schedule. Coarse localisation survives propagation; fine localisation does not. Preprint. (arXiv 2509.18388, 2025; https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18388; read: full for abstract + intro) [Huang, Wang, Yao, Dev]

**Verdict Q1.** Keyframe detection plus cheap propagation is a standard, well-quantified idea: 3-5x compute savings for a few points of accuracy (DFF), content-adaptive triggers beat fixed intervals (DorT, Glimpse, MARLIN, Reducto), and Kalman forecasting recovers much of the accuracy lost to latency (sAP). The trade-off is always accuracy-vs-keyframe-distance, with failures at newly appearing or abruptly changing objects. All of these evaluate detection/box quality, not a downstream temporal model, and none is on a CPU-only pose pipeline. The transferable lesson is that the schedule should be driven by a cheap tracking-quality/scene-change signal calibrated against the downstream metric.

## Q2. Pose-specific propagation, interpolation and completion

- Key Frame Proposal Network: an unsupervised light network picks informative key frames, the pose estimator runs only there, and a learned dynamics-based dictionary (Human Pose Interpolation Module) fills the other frames (a B-frame analogue, offline) -> 1.6x faster than the previous best (6.8 vs 11 ms) with 17.5 key frames on average on Penn Action, 2x faster with the lightest backbone at "minor" PCK loss; claimed robust to occlusion/blur/poor lighting in the skipped frames. Closest pose-level "keyframes + interpolation" work, but it needs the whole clip (non-causal) and is measured by PCK. (ECCV 2020; https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.15217, https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/papers/123620596.pdf; read: full for the abstract + speed passage) [Zhang, Wang, Camps, Sznaier]
- FVPE: distilled small pose estimator plus explicit propagation of joints from the previous key frame, with an online key-frame selection scheme that decides per frame to estimate or propagate -> "comparative accuracy but substantial speed-up" on Penn Action and Sub-JHMDB (no numbers in the abstract). A causal "P" scheme with adaptive keyframes for pose. (IJCNN 2022; https://doi.org/10.1109/ijcnn55064.2022.9892706; read: abstract) [Zhou, Yu, Xu et al.; first names not checked]
- PosePropagationNet: transforms the previous frame's pose by learned joint motion offsets to get the current pose, so a lightweight network only has to refine -> 2.5x and 6x FLOPs reduction (PPN-Stable / PPN-Swift) with higher accuracy than the previous best on Penn Action and Sub-JHMDB. Learned causal propagation; needs a per-frame lightweight network, not zero compute. (IEEE Access 2020; https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/ielx7/6287639/8948470/09103077.pdf; read: abstract + method excerpt; authors per an aggregator header: Liu, Chen) 
- Dynamic Kernel Distillation: distils pose kernels from the previous frame so a small network localises joints by matching -> 10x FLOPs reduction and 2x speed-up over the previous best at state-of-the-art accuracy (Penn Action, Sub-JHMDB). Same family (per-frame small net conditioned on the past). (ICCV 2019; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Nie_Dynamic_Kernel_Distillation_for_Efficient_Pose_Estimation_in_Videos_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf; read: abstract) [Nie et al.; author list not checked]
- PoseWarper: learns deformable-conv pose warping between a labelled and an unlabelled frame so poses can be propagated from sparse labelled frames to the rest -> 88.7% mAP for propagation against 83.8% for optical-flow label propagation. A learned bidirectional (B-like) pose propagation, but it uses the pixels of both frames, so it is not free. (NeurIPS 2019; https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04016; read: abstract; authors not checked)
- Thin-Slicing Network: warps per-frame joint heat-maps to neighbouring frames with dense optical flow and runs spatio-temporal inference -> the authors state they deliberately use no explicit motion model "since human motion tends to be too unpredictable" and rely on flow. Evidence against a pure constant-velocity predictor for long gaps. (CVPR 2017; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Song_Thin-Slicing_Network_A_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf; read: full for the quoted sentence; authors per URL: Song et al.)
- On human motion prediction using RNNs: shows that a zero-velocity baseline (repeat the last pose) and 2/4-frame running averages beat the then state-of-the-art RNNs for short-term mocap prediction -> e.g. Walking error 0.39/0.68/0.99/1.15 at 80/160/320/400 ms for zero-velocity against 0.77-1.29 for LSTM-3LR (copied from Table 1; Human3.6M, mocap, not detector output). A trivial predictor is hard to beat at the short horizons we need; deterministic losses are unsuited to long horizons. (CVPR 2017; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Martinez_On_Human_Motion_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf; read: full for Table 1 + conclusion) [Martinez, Black, Romero]
- Convolutional autoencoders for human motion infilling: casts filling a gap between a start and end sequence as image inpainting on joint-position maps; also recovers joints missing over a whole sequence -> a B-frame analogue for 3D mocap; needs training on motion capture. (arXiv 2010.11531; https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11531; read: abstract + one experiment paragraph; authors/venue not checked)
- AuxFormer: transformer for 3D motion prediction trained with masked-joint and denoising auxiliary tasks -> more robust when joints/frames are missing; and PORT: masked joint modelling as a plug-in refiner for occluded joints on top of any 2D keypoint detector, up to 16% better with about 5% extra parameters. Masked-joint modelling is the standard learned imputation recipe. (arXiv 2308.08942, https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08942; ICRA 2023, https://doi.org/10.1109/icra48891.2023.10161259; read: abstract) [AuxFormer authors not checked; PORT: not checked]
- FLK: Kalman filter plus low-pass filter whose motion model is learned by an RNN, with biomechanical spatial constraints, for zero/low-latency 3D pose refinement -> handles jitter and dropped frames; up to 53 mm better with missing information. A learned-Kalman P-predictor precedent, 3D and not tested on 2D detector gaps. (journal/venue not confirmed; https://iris.univr.it/handle/11562/1131906; read: abstract)
- Multi-person fall detection with a Kalman filter: puts keypoint confidence into the Kalman measurement covariance, ignores low-confidence measurements, and lets a joint run on prediction-only for a bounded number of time steps -> the practical recipe for a confidence-weighted, bounded-extrapolation P-predictor; in this paper the filter improves fall-detection correctness. (IEEE Access 2025; https://selab.hongik.ac.kr/selab/data/papers/2025_IEEE_Access_1_%EC%96%91%EC%A7%84%EB%AA%A8.pdf; read: abstract + method excerpt; authors not checked)
- 1 euro filter: first-order low-pass filter whose cut-off rises with signal speed -> at low speed it removes jitter, at high speed it reduces lag; RTMPose uses it, plus detection every K frames, in its inference pipeline. The standard answer to "smoothing adds lag on fast motion". (CHI 2012; https://gery.casiez.net/publications/CHI2012-casiez.pdf; read: abstract + intro) [Casiez et al., per URL; other authors not checked]
- Weak evidence: first-order temporal interpolation of one missing keypoint (ankle) in monocular gait video cut knee-angle error from 23.4 +/- 46.7 to 1.1 +/- 6.7 degrees on 527 frames of one walking video. Single video, preprint. (arXiv 2609.09670, 2026; https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09670; read: abstract)
- Practitioner note (not peer reviewed): a MediaPipe issue reports that during explosive movements, landmarks smoothed/predicted by the tracker detach from the motion, more visibly at low FPS (https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/issues/6239; read: issue text). Anecdote for the "over-smooth / lag on sudden motion" risk only.

**Verdict Q2.** Pose propagation between sparse keyframes is established (K-FPN, FVPE, PPN, DKD, PoseWarper), and adaptive key-frame selection exists at pose level (FVPE, K-FPN). They report PCK/mAP on Penn Action and Sub-JHMDB (single, centred persons, short clips) and use learned per-frame networks or the clip's full future; none uses a zero-compute motion model on top of a multi-person detector+tracker output, none measures downstream anomaly scoring. Simple predictors (zero-velocity, running average, first-order interpolation) are strong at 80-400 ms, while flow/learned methods are used where gaps are longer or motion is hard. Evidence that predictors under-represent sudden motion is indirect (Thin-Slicing remark, 1-euro lag trade-off, the MediaPipe anecdote), not measured on falls or fights.

## Q3. Skeleton-based anomaly detection with missing, occluded or interpolated poses

- STG-NF released code (segmenter and scorer): `is_seg_continuous(..., missing_th=2)` accepts a 24-frame (or `seg_len`) window if at most 2 frame indices are missing, and the window is then built from the next `seg_len` stored pose rows without imputing the missing frames (the time axis is silently compressed); window scores are assigned to the frame at `start + seg_len/2` (so scoring needs about 12 future frames), and the frame-level scores are Gaussian-smoothed with sigma = 7 (`smooth_scores`). The released scorer therefore already tolerates tiny gaps, not larger ones, and already has an inherent look-ahead of half a window. Verify against our own fork before using these facts. (ICCV 2023 code; https://github.com/orhir/STG-NF, utils/pose_utils.py and utils/scoring_utils.py; read: code) [Hirschorn & Avidan]
- TrajREC: trajectory-based multitask anomaly model on skeletons -> its public reconstruction script has an `--input_missing_steps` option that fills missing trajectory steps with a weighted combination of the nearest non-missing steps; no robustness numbers found. The only skeleton-VAD codebase I found with built-in imputation. (arXiv 2311.01851; https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01851, https://github.com/alexandrosstergiou/TrajREC; read: code argparse help via search excerpt + abstract; venue not confirmed) [Stergiou, De Weerdt, Deligiannis]
- MoCoDAD: diffusion model that generates multimodal future poses conditioned on the past and flags an anomaly when generated poses are not pertinent to the real future -> no missing-keypoint or frame-drop experiment in the parts I read; the model depends on a clean past window. (ICCV 2023; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Flaborea_Multimodal_Motion_Conditioned_Diffusion_Model_for_Skeleton-based_Video_Anomaly_Detection_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf; read: abstract + intro; no robustness test seen) [Flaborea et al.]
- SeeKer: sequential keypoint density estimator that weights per-keypoint evidence by detector confidence -> uses AlphaPose + YOLOX + re-ID, and says that extractors/trackers give unsatisfactory output on UCF-Crime and Avenue so those datasets are skipped; confidence is used inside the score, with no gap or interpolation study. Confirms that coverage failures are a known blocker. (ICCV 2025; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Delic_Sequential_keypoint_density_estimator_an_overlooked_baseline_of_skeleton-based_video_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf; read: full for the dataset-skipping and extractor passage) [Delic, Grcic, Segvic]
- Reliability-aware prototype calibration for frozen pose-flow detectors: post-hoc score calibration that gates the added evidence by keypoint confidence; AUROC up +0.34 to +4.49 points (mean +2.03) across two frozen backbones and four datasets; perturbation protocol includes keypoint dropout (per LITCHECK-11-04). Provides the precedent for gating or re-weighting a frozen STG-NF score by pose reliability. Preprint. (arXiv 2606.20312, 2026; https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20312; read: abstract (arXiv page)) [Dong, Su, Dong, Jiao, Guo, Pan]
- STEP: PCA-whitened score-based energy model with sequence-level weighting by the pose estimator's confidence -> 90.1% AUROC on UBnormal; the authors argue that weighting mitigates occlusion/blur pose errors; no interpolation. Preprint. (arXiv 2608.19987, 2026; https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19987; read: abstract + efficiency passage)
- Action-Hints skeleton VAD: states the convention that a frame with no detected person is scored normal -> same convention as our pipeline. (arXiv 2509.11058; https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11058; read: snippet only)
- SHARDeg (skeleton action recognition, not VAD, but the closest degradation study): drops frames uniformly, randomly or as one block on NTU-120 across five models -> at the same effective rate the accuracy varies by more than 40% with the degradation type (DeGCN at 90% dropout: 55% uniform, 42% random, 16% single block); replacing dropped frames by linear interpolation of all joints from the adjacent available frames restores up to >40% for MS-G3D at 80% dropout, but only +0.8% on average for the models that already resample inputs (FR-HEAD, GCN-Dev-LSTM, DeGCN). Says temporal regularity of the surviving frames drives much of the effect; interpolation helps models that cannot cope with irregular spacing. (arXiv 2505.18048, 2025; https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18048; read: full for abstract, mitigation and results passages) [Malzard, Mital et al.]
- FineTec: completes temporally corrupted 2D skeleton sequences (25/50/75% frames dropped) before fine-grained action recognition -> beats duplication and interpolation baselines, e.g. on Gym288 Top-1 0.815 / 0.797 / 0.778 at minor / moderate / severe corruption, with the largest advantage under severe corruption; the intro says online pose estimation can drop up to 69.6% of frames during rapid motion (citing another work, not checked). Learned completion beats linear interpolation when most frames are missing. (AAAI (year not confirmed, OJS download); https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/37838/41800; read: abstract + intro + results excerpt) [Shao, Shi, Liu]
- Realistic augmentation for skeleton action recognition: simulates missing keypoints (low confidence), fragmented tracks and, explicitly, artefacts of temporal up-sampling ("interpolation artefacts if the chosen key frames are erroneous") during training -> improves robustness on UAV-Human and JHMDB. Precedent for training the scorer on predicted-pose noise. (WACV Workshops 2024; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024W/RWS/papers/Cormier_Enhancing_Skeleton-Based_Action_Recognition_in_Real-World_Scenarios_Through_Realistic_Data_WACVW_2024_paper.pdf; read: abstract + intro) [Cormier, Schmid, Beyerer]
- Already covered (not repeated): STG-NF paper itself, IJIT 2024 pose-selection paper (recovers incomplete poses to avoid lost tracking; 86.4 vs 85.9 AUC), Huang 2026 Pi 5 pipeline, skeleton-VAD survey (arXiv 2301.00114: missed joints raise false alarms).

**Verdict Q3.** I found no skeleton-VAD paper that imputes poses, interpolates across dropped frames, or evaluates AUC under frame drops/irregular sampling; the existing robustness work is keypoint noise/dropout and confidence weighting (SeeKer, STEP, RPC). The nearest evidence comes from skeleton action recognition: irregular dropping hurts far more than uniform dropping (SHARDeg), linear interpolation is a strong, simple fix for models that cannot handle irregular spacing, and learned completion wins only at severe corruption (FineTec). STG-NF's own released segmenter tolerates at most 2 missing frames per window and does not impute, so a gap-filling front end is a precisely open slot for it. Caveat: action recognition is supervised and event-level; a density model scoring frame-level anomalies may react differently to interpolated (smoother) poses, which no source tests.

## Q4. Codec-inspired and motion-vector tricks; I/P/B for pose streams

- Fast Human Pose Estimation in Compressed Videos: top-down pose estimator (HRNet) on I-frames, joints of P-frames warped by the codec motion vectors, a pose-recall module that re-runs the estimator on "hard-to-warp" people (decided from motion intensity and joint confidence), and a transition re-initialisation module for scene cuts -> 2-5x speed-up at comparable accuracy; warping alone drops mAP from 79.2 to 54.1, with recall + re-initialisation it is 77.2 at nearly 4x speed-up; MV warping is 3-8x faster than optical flow; the whole framework 3-5x faster than per-frame because detection is only needed on I-frames. The single closest work to a pose "I + P" scheme, with an explicit safety valve for drift. P only (no B), benchmark accuracy only. (IEEE TMM 2022; https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~wayang/pdf/tmm22_pose.pdf; read: full for the abstract, ablation and speed passages) [Liu, Liu, Chi, Wang, Yu, Chen, Tang]
- Motion Adaptive Pose Net: MPEG-4 GOP (I-frame + 11 P-frames), propagates features with a motion-compensated ConvLSTM and a residual-driven gate that decides when to compute expensive features on a decoded frame -> better accuracy than the previous best at about half the computation (Penn Action, Sub-JHMDB). Adaptive keyframe decision driven by the codec residual. (ICCV 2021; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/Fan_Motion_Adaptive_Pose_Estimation_From_Compressed_Videos_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf; read: full for abstract + method intro) [Fan, Liu, Wang]
- Fast Object Detection in Compressed Video: detection features propagated with motion vectors and residuals, I-frame based -> 3x faster than single-image R-FCN and 10x faster than MANet at a minor accuracy loss (ImageNet VID). Same idea for boxes. (ICCV 2019; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Wang_Fast_Object_Detection_in_Compressed_Video_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf; read: abstract) [Wang, Lu, Deng]
- See Without Decoding: detects boxes on the I-frame only and propagates them through P-frames with a model over motion vectors and DCT residuals -> up to 3.7x speed-up with a 4% mAP@0.5 drop against the RGB baseline on MOTS15/17/20. Preprint-like (ESANN 2026 proceedings). (arXiv 2602.00153; https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00153; read: abstract) [authors not checked]
- CoVA: compressed-domain blob detection and tracking, then DNNs only on "anchor frames" chosen by a frame-selection algorithm that accounts for codec dependency chains (decode cost) and the number of tracks covered -> keeps labels for whole tracks from few decoded frames; a precedent for choosing keyframes by both usefulness and cost. (arXiv 2207.00588; https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00588; read: abstract + method excerpt; venue not confirmed) [Hwang, Kim, Kim, Nam, Kim, Kim, Sharma, Park]
- MVmed tracker (code, not peer reviewed): runs a detector at regular intervals and, between them, predicts each box by averaging motion vectors inside it; it is explicitly not limited to key frames and can use P and B frames; README reports no accuracy numbers I could check. (https://github.com/lukasbommes/mvmed-tracker; read: README)
- Extending Neural P-frame codecs for B-frame coding: interpolates the two reference frames and uses that as the P-frame reference -> the interpolated reference is a much better reference than the previous frame, with 28.5% bit-rate saving on UVG. Confirms the codec intuition (bidirectional > forward) on pixels, not poses. (ICCV 2021; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/Pourreza_Extending_Neural_P-Frame_Codecs_for_B-Frame_Coding_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf; read: abstract; authors per URL: Pourreza et al.)
- Real-time compression of human time-varying meshes: I-frames, P-frames and enhanced-P frames chosen per frame by a "skeletal motion" threshold (sum of per-bone shifts relative to the last I-frame) -> the only skeleton-driven I/P scheme I found, but it compresses meshes for storage/streaming, with no recognition or anomaly task. (https://vcl.iti.gr/media/documents/towards-real-time-and-efficient-compression-of-human-time-varying-meshes.pdf; read: method excerpt; venue/authors not checked)
- Already in LITREVIEW: Baba et al. 2019 (MPEG motion vectors, violence detection on a Raspberry Pi).

**Verdict Q4.** Codec-style I/P propagation is established for boxes (several papers) and for pose estimation in compressed video (Liu 2022, Fan 2021), with a re-detect/recall mechanism that the 2022 paper shows is essential (54.1 vs 77.2 mAP). B-style (bidirectional) use exists for pose only as offline key-frame interpolation (K-FPN) and for pixel codecs. I found no paper that applies an I/P/B scheme to a pose stream for video anomaly detection, edge analytics under deadlines, or tracker-gap filling; the nearest "skeleton-driven I/P" is mesh compression. If the camera's H.264 bit-stream is accessible, motion vectors give a nearly free P-predictor (Liu 2022), but the benchmark videos (ShanghaiTech, UBnormal) were not encoded for this, so the first study should use pose-only prediction.

## Q5. Deadline-aware pose estimation on edge; frame skipping coupled to downstream temporal models

- AdaHPE: edge human pose estimation that turns per-frame regression into a sequence of pose-propagation tasks, uses a memory-augmented LSTM to pick key frames from the data and the device's resource status, and uses RL (policy gradient) to adjust the key-frame ratio under accuracy/latency/resource rewards -> reduces computation against per-frame HPE on Penn Action, Sub-JHMDB, NTU RGB+D 120 and real-world data while keeping accuracy "under varying resource limitations"; compatible with off-the-shelf HPE models. The closest published work to "deadline-/resource-aware keyframe pose with propagation"; abstract only, so I cannot say how it fails on gaps or whether it feeds a downstream model. (IEEE Internet of Things Journal 12(22) 2025; https://doi.org/10.1109/jiot.2025.3603175; read: abstract) [Zhou, Hu, Jia, Xie; first names not checked]
- LightTrack: online top-down pose tracking with two keyframe modes, Fixed Keyframe Interval (a lost target is ignored until the next scheduled keyframe, stable frame rate) and Adaptive Keyframe Interval (revive a lost target immediately by detection, higher average frame rate on simple videos); the next-frame ROI is the enlarged box of the current joints. A direct description of fixed vs adaptive pose keyframe schedules and their latency-stability trade-off; it propagates the person box, but still runs the single-person pose network on every frame. (arXiv 1905.02822; https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02822; read: full for the FKI/AKI paragraph; authors not checked)
- BlazePose and RTMPose: skip the person detector and reuse boxes from the previous pose (BlazePose runs the detector only when the tracker says the person is gone; RTMPose runs it every K frames and adds pose NMS plus a one-euro filter) -> over 30 FPS on a Pixel 2 (BlazePose); RTMPose-s 72.2% AP, 70+ FPS on Snapdragon 865. Frame skipping in production pose stacks skips the detector, not the pose network. Our YOLOv8n-pose is single-stage, so a skipped frame skips both. (arXiv 2006.10204, https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10204; arXiv 2303.07399, https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07399; read: abstract + pipeline passages) [author lists not checked]
- Towards Streaming Perception (above, Q1): the deadline-vs-accuracy formalism, with Kalman forecasting; "it is OK to skip frames" in the authors' repo notes. (read: see Q1)

**Verdict Q5.** Resource-aware keyframe scheduling for pose exists on edge (AdaHPE), as do fixed-vs-adaptive keyframe modes (LightTrack) and a deadline-aware metric with forecasting (sAP). The coupling of frame drops to a downstream temporal skeleton model is studied for action recognition (SHARDeg, UCF101 frame-drop) and shows that how frames are dropped matters as much as how many. ## Synthesis for the owner's idea

### (a) What is already done (closest three, and how close)

1. **Fast Human Pose Estimation in Compressed Videos** (IEEE TMM 2022, https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~wayang/pdf/tmm22_pose.pdf) [Liu et al.]. Very close in mechanism for "I + P": detect on I-frames, warp joints on P-frames, re-detect when the warp is unreliable, 2-5x faster. It differs in using codec motion vectors, in being evaluated only by pose mAP on single-person benchmarks, and in having no B-frames, no deadline notion, no tracker/coverage view and no anomaly scorer.
2. **AdaHPE** (IEEE IoT-J 2025, https://doi.org/10.1109/jiot.2025.3603175) [Zhou et al.]. Close in goal: edge, resource-aware, adaptive keyframes, propagation in between. Differs by being learned (LSTM + RL), single-person HPE benchmarks, and (from the abstract) no downstream model or gap/track analysis. Abstract only: re-check the full paper through the RMIT library before claiming differences.
3. **Key Frame Proposal Network** (ECCV 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.15217) [Zhang et al.]. Close for "B-frames": detect on selected key frames and interpolate the rest with a dynamics model. Differs by being offline/non-causal, clip-based, and PCK-evaluated.

Runners-up that cover single ingredients: Towards Streaming Perception (Kalman prediction to cover latency, boxes; https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10420), SHARDeg (linear interpolation of dropped frames helps skeleton models; https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18048), FineTec (learned completion beats interpolation when most frames are missing; AAAI OJS 37838), and the released STG-NF code (tolerates 2 missing frames per window; https://github.com/orhir/STG-NF).

### (b) What appears novel (precise and honest)

Not novel (do not claim): keyframe detection plus propagation; adaptive keyframes; motion-vector pose propagation; Kalman forecasting for deadline gaps; linear interpolation of dropped skeleton frames improves a downstream skeleton model (shown for action recognition).

Appears novel, for the sources I could reach (no Scholar pass; absence is "not found"):
1. Using predicted poses (P/B) to repair **tracker gaps and deadline-dropped frames in a pose-based video-anomaly pipeline** and measuring the effect on frame-level AUC of a density-model scorer (STG-NF). I found no skeleton-VAD paper that imputes poses or reports AUC under frame drops.
2. Framing the benefit through **coverage and track continuity** (median track length, share of frames with a scoreable window), and showing that predicted poses raise both; the mechanism (fragmented tracks -> no scoreable 24-frame window) is your own finding from EXP-003/004/008.
3. **Delivered AUC under a real-time deadline** as the evaluation, with P/B frames as the remedy for deadline drops. sAP is the nearest metric precedent, but for detection.
4. A **B-frame observation specific to window scorers**: the released STG-NF assigns each window's score to its central frame, so scoring already needs about 12 future frames; bounded look-ahead for B-interpolation (key-frame gaps up to a few frames) then adds no extra latency beyond the scorer's own delay (derived from the released code; verify in our pipeline). Generic I/P/B papers treat B-latency as a cost.
5. A direct test of the **anomaly-hiding concern** (does prediction suppress falls/punches?), which the pose-propagation papers never test because they have no anomaly labels.

Honest framing: this is an application/system study, not a new propagation algorithm. The novelty claim should be "first measured effect of pose I/P/B propagation on edge pose-VAD under deadlines", with an explicit related-work paragraph on Liu 2022, AdaHPE, K-FPN and SHARDeg. Also note the baseline already scores person-free frames as normal, so predicted poses cannot hide anomalies more than the baseline does in frames where nothing was tracked; the hiding risk is real only where prediction replaces a pose that would otherwise have been detected (a deliberate keyframe schedule, goal 3).

### (c) Recommended design choices from the literature

- **Predictor.** Start with the simplest: B = per-joint linear interpolation between two detected keyframes (SHARDeg; gait study; Martinez shows trivial predictors are strong at 80-400 ms); P = zero-velocity or damped constant-velocity on the person-box centre with the shape (joint offsets relative to the box) held from the last detected pose, so limb lengths stay valid (my design inference, to test). Upgrade to a confidence-weighted Kalman (measurement covariance from keypoint confidence, bounded prediction-only steps; IEEE Access 2025 fall paper; asynchronous time-varying Kalman of sAP) only if constant velocity overshoots on stops. Move to learned predictors (K-FPN dictionary, PPN, FineTec-like completion, masked-joint transformers) only if the linear versions fail at longer gaps; FineTec's advantage over interpolation appears at 75% drop, not at mild drop. Thin-Slicing's remark and the 54.1 vs 77.2 result show that unguarded long extrapolation breaks.
- **Bound the prediction.** Cap consecutive P frames (start with <= 4 frames, about 170 ms at 24 fps, inside the 80-400 ms range Martinez evaluates; tune empirically) and re-detect on a trigger (Liu 2022's recall module: motion intensity + joint confidence; DorT/AdaHPE learned decisions; Glimpse's tracking-quality and scene-change triggers). B-interpolation only between two detected keyframes with gap <= the cap.
- **Keyframe schedule.** Baseline = fixed interval K (LightTrack FKI) as the compute-saving mode; adaptive = shorten the interval when joint speed or box-IoU(predicted, detected) indicates fast motion or drift, and add a cheap frame-difference trigger so a newly entering person is not missed for up to K frames (MARLIN's failure mode; Glimpse trigger; Reducto warns that a static threshold must be calibrated against the downstream metric). For deadline drops, treat any late/dropped frame as a P frame and fill when the next detection arrives (sAP-style async handling).
- **Marking predicted vs detected poses.** Keep a per-frame (and per-joint) provenance flag, plus an age since last detection and a decayed confidence, exactly as the Kalman-covariance literature does. The released STG-NF supports a confidence input channel only through `--model_confidence` at training time (utils/train_utils.py), so with the frozen checkpoint mark at **score level**: (i) require a minimum share of detected frames in a window, (ii) down-weight/gate windows by predicted share (RPC and SeeKer/STEP gate or weight by keypoint confidence), (iii) report AUC with and without predicted-dominated windows. If the frozen scorer shows a distribution shift, fine-tune STG-NF on normal data with synthetic P/B noise (Cormier et al. simulate temporal up-sampling artefacts; training stays unsupervised).
- **Avoid smoothing away anomalies.** Never filter detected keyframe poses before scoring; apply any smoother only to predicted frames or use a speed-adaptive (1-euro-style) filter; keep keyframe gaps short where speed is high so the impulsive part (a fall's onset) is observed rather than interpolated; prefer B-interpolation (bounded by two real detections) over long P extrapolation; keep the score smoothing as released (sigma = 7 already blurs the score over about 14 frames, so artefacts shorter than that are averaged, a point to verify in our fork).

### (d) Risks and how to measure them

1. **Hidden or attenuated anomalies.** Measure AUC separately on anomalous frames that are in predicted spans vs detected spans; peak-score attenuation (max window NLL with predicted vs oracle poses); anomaly-onset detection delay; per-anomaly-type breakdown (falls, fights, bikes) on ShanghaiTech and UBnormal.
2. **Unnatural poses -> false positives or flow-likelihood shift.** Compare NLL distributions of predicted vs detected windows on normal test data; joint jerk/second-difference energy and bone-length variance of predicted vs detected poses; check that predicted-heavy windows are not systematically scored more normal or more abnormal than detected ones.
3. **Late or missed new persons (sparse detection).** Track-start delay, share of person-frames covered, median track length (target: toward the 118 of the heavy pipeline from 61).
4. **Ghost poses after a person leaves, or ID switches during long predictions.** Count predicted frames with no ground-truth person and identity switches vs a no-prediction baseline.
5. **No net gain in time.** Account for predictor and trigger overhead against the saved detection time; measure delivered AUC at the 24 fps deadline, including B look-ahead in the latency budget; confirm on the Pi 5, not only the VM.
6. **Evaluation artefacts.** Predicted frames use future frames (B); report latency honestly; do not tune K on the test labels (use training-set normals or a validation split).

### (e) Suggested experiment list (cheap first)

1. **E0 (no new inference, minutes of CPU).** Take the cached YOLOv8n-pose tracks (EXP-003 outputs). Simulate keyframe schedules K in {2, 3, 4, 6, 8} by deleting the non-key poses, fill with {nothing, zero-velocity, constant velocity, linear B, Kalman}, score with the frozen STG-NF; plot AUC and coverage vs K. Repeat on the released AlphaPose poses (SHARDeg-style) to separate predictor effects from front-end effects.
2. **E1 (gap filling only).** Fill gaps <= g frames inside existing tracks (g in {2, 4, 8, 12}) with linear interpolation; report median track length, window coverage and AUC. Check how the segmenter treats the gaps (the released one allows 2).
3. **E2 (headline).** Re-run the EXP-008 delivered-AUC replay: for deadline-dropped frames, fill with P (causal) or B (bounded look-ahead) and report delivered AUC at s in {1, 2, 4, 8} on both datasets.
4. **E3 (anomaly-hiding diagnostics).** Risk 1-2 metrics above on E0/E2 outputs; no new compute.
5. **E4 (marking ablation).** Window gating/weighting by predicted share and confidence decay; with vs without predicted-dominated windows.
6. **E5 (adaptive triggers).** Fixed K vs adaptive (box-IoU, joint speed, frame-difference for new persons) with real timing including trigger overhead.
7. **E6 (only if E0 shows distribution shift).** Fine-tune STG-NF on normal training windows with synthetic P/B noise.
8. **E7 (only if linear fails).** A small learned predictor on joint trajectories or an FineTec-style completion; or codec MV warping if the bit-stream is accessible (measure MV extraction cost on the Pi).
9. **E8.** Pi 5 timing confirmation of the best configuration.
