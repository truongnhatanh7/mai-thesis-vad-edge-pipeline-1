## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. **P2 head for YOLO-pose is not novel as an architecture**; the combination "P2 + site pseudo-labels that include sub-32-px people + evaluation by person height + downstream VAD AUC priced under a CPU deadline" is not something I found in any paper. Novelty is *moderate and lives in the diagnosis and the pricing, not in the head* (section A5).
2. **Ultralytics ships no P2 pose config and no P2 weights.** Installed 8.4.201 has `v8/yolov8-p2.yaml` (detect), `26/yolo26-p2.yaml` (detect), `v8/yolov8-pose-p6.yaml`, and no `*-pose-p2*` anywhere (checked `[L]`). The docs say the YOLO26 P2/P6 YAMLs are architecture-only with no weights. A P2 pose model must be made by hand (swap the last layer to `Pose`) and, if built naively, its heads are **trained from scratch** (section A4).
3. **Strong mechanism for the recall cliff, and it is about labels more than stride.** COCO has no keypoint labels for persons with segment area < 32^2; pycocotools ignores them in evaluation; the installed Ultralytics COCO converter *drops them from the label files*, so a COCO-pose-trained model is shown them as background `[L]`. EXP-013 already closed 11.8 of the 17.4-AUC gap with stride-8 only, using labels. A P2 head can therefore explain at most the remaining ~5.7 AUC (80.28 vs 85.94, SHT) and only the very smallest people.
4. **P2 costs about what the 800-px knob costs**: at rect 384x640, +67% GFLOPs (5.58 -> 9.34 `[L]`) versus stock at rect 480x800 = 8.71 GFLOPs `[L]` (+56%). So P2@640 is an iso-cost competitor of "NCNN @ 800", which EXP-012 already showed wins on delivered AUC. P2 must beat that, not the 640 baseline.
5. **TSM verdict:** zero-parameter temporal mixing is **useless inside STG-NF's coupling subnet** (it has 2 channels and a 13-tap temporal conv that is ~70% of 616 parameters; scorer costs ~0.94 ms/window; GAU already showed that more temporal capacity is not the bottleneck). The only TSM idea with a plausible payoff is **online TSM in the detector backbone with a stride-randomised fine-tune**, and it is worth doing only if a free diagnostic (B0) shows missed small people are *intermittent*, not *never seen*. Expected value is below the P2/label-policy experiments.
6. **Recommended order (cheapest first):** A0/B0 free diagnostics on existing caches -> A1 label-policy ablation (one fine-tune) -> A2 P2 append-only vs stock@800 (iso-cost) -> B1 only if B0 passes. Details in sections A6 and B6.

## A. P2 head for small-person detection/pose

### A1. Plain-language explanation

A YOLO detector makes predictions on grids ("feature maps") at several resolutions. The grid at **stride 8** (P3) has one cell per 8x8 image pixels; stride 16 (P4) and 32 (P5) are coarser. YOLOv8-pose predicts boxes and 17 keypoints from P3, P4, P5. A **P2 head** adds a fourth, finer grid at **stride 4** (one cell per 4x4 pixels), built by upsampling the neck and mixing in a shallow, high-resolution backbone layer. Two things change:
- *Features*: a small person gets 4x as many cells, with less downsampling before the prediction.
- *Training signal*: Ultralytics' assigner (`TaskAlignedAssigner`) only lets anchors whose **centre lies inside the ground-truth box** become positives for that person (`select_candidates_in_gts`, installed `utils/tal.py` `[L]`). A person that is 24 px tall x 10 px wide at model scale covers about 3 x 1.3 stride-8 cells (~3-4 centres, often 0-2 after the in-box test) but ~6 x 2.5 stride-4 cells (~15 centres) `[D]`. Few positives means the person is effectively un-learnable at P3 even *with* labels. `[I]` on how the assigner behaves for sub-3-cell boxes; the in-box rule itself is read from code.

Cost: the finest grid is large (at rect 384x640, 96x160 = 15,360 cells vs 3,840 at P3; total anchors 20,400 vs 5,040 `[D]`), and in YOLO-pose each cell carries a 51-channel keypoint branch, so the P2 pose branch is expensive.

### A3. How a P2 pose model would be trained on our stack

- **Weight loading is the practical catch.** Ultralytics loads weights by matching name and shape. In `yolov8-p2.yaml` the neck layers after index 15 differ from `yolov8-pose.yaml` and the per-level head modules are indexed by level, so levels shift (P2 becomes level 0). I expect only the backbone (layers 0-9) and neck layers 12 and 15 to load; **the P3-P5 head convolutions and the PAN-down layers would be re-initialised** `[I]` (derived by comparing the two YAMLs; not run). The released COCO-pose head knowledge would be lost, and fine-tuning on ~13 cameras of pseudo-labels (EXP-013 scale) would have to relearn it. This matches the KomTekInfo negative result.
- **Safer construction (my proposal, untested): append-only P2.** Keep layers 0-21 of stock `yolov8n-pose` unchanged, add a new branch (upsample layer 15 -> concat backbone layer 2 -> C2f[128]) and give the `Pose` head the level list [P3, P4, P5, P2] so that levels 0-2 keep their pretrained heads and only the P2 head modules are new. Anchors and strides are computed per input tensor in the head, so level order should not matter `[I]`. Pre-flight gate: the P3-P5 outputs of the new model must equal the stock model's to ~1e-5 on a frame before any training; if they do not, stop.
- COCO-pose from-scratch training of a P2 head (COCO has no small-person keypoints anyway) is not needed and not affordable on a Mac.
- Labels: EXP-013 already uses all AlphaPose persons with no height filter (box = keypoint extents + 10%, visibility from conf > 0.3; `EXP-013/plan.md` `[L]`). AlphaPose keypoints for sub-32-px people are noisy; the recall metric of EXP-011 is also noisy for them (matcher bias noted there).
- Export: ORT export of a 4-level pose head is standard; NCNN export of the 4-level head is not verified.

### A4. Does COCO lack small-person keypoint labels? (yes), and how it maps to our cliff

Facts: COCO annotates keypoints only for people above 32^2 segment area; others have `num_keypoints = 0` (COCO page, pycocotools, converter above). Evaluation ignores such people (page), and the installed converter removes them from the labels (code), so training sees them as background `[L]`. **Rough height equivalent `[D]` with my own assumption** that a standing person's segment area is ~0.27 h^2 (width ~0.38 h, fill ~0.7): area < 1024 means h < ~62 px at COCO scale (images are ~640 px wide, model scale ~1). Our frames are 856x480, scaled by 0.75 to 640 wide, so the same cutoff would sit at ~83 px in original pixels. EXP-011 measured recall 0.06 (48-64 px), 0.61 (64-96 px), 0.96 (96-160 px) at 640 `[M, local]`; the cliff is in the right region, though not at a sharp 83 px. So: (a) the detector has been taught to ignore, or never seen, the people we miss; (b) the best intervention is *giving it positives*, which EXP-013 did (coverage 0.674 -> 0.952 `[local]`) at stride 8, so label policy, not stride, is the first-order cause `[I]`; (c) stride matters only when the person is too small for stride-8 anchors to be assigned.

**Gap left for a P2 head, from our own numbers:** after fine-tuning SHT AUC is 80.28 vs the 85.94 reference (EXP-013) and oracle boxes + RTMPose give ~86 (EXP-015). So at most ~5.7 AUC remains on ShanghaiTech and it is not known how much of it is sub-36-px (model scale) people. EXP-011 shows 40% of reference persons are < 48 px tall (original px), with recall 0.001-0.007 at 640 *before* fine-tuning; we have no recall-by-height for the fine-tuned model yet (cheap, free gate A0 below).

### A5. Novelty verdict

- **Architecture:** adding P2 to YOLO-pose is known and low-novelty. Community advice since 2024 (Ultralytics issue above), three application papers bundle a P2/small-object head or P2-level fusion into YOLOv8-pose and report COCO val results (EE-YOLOv8, fcst 2025, PoseBoost); KSL-POSE explicitly avoids an extra head for cost. **None of them can measure the small-person benefit**, because COCO's small persons have no keypoints; I found no paper that evaluates a P2 pose head by person height on a surveillance or tiny-person pose benchmark (web search only).
- **Teacher pseudo-labels for a small-person pose student:** closest are DA-LLPose (ECCV 2024, missed persons, low light), PedSense (card), and the BMVC 2024 / Scenes100 box-only scene adaptation. I found no paper doing it with AlphaPose-class teacher labels for **pose-based video anomaly detection** (also stated in the prior lane).
- **Is "YOLO-pose + P2 + pseudo-labels for surveillance small people" novel?** As a *recipe*, incremental: each ingredient exists. What I could not find and would defend as the contribution: (1) the mechanism (COCO keypoint annotation policy -> recall cliff by person height, measured, EXP-011); (2) the causal ablation of *label policy vs stride vs resolution* on the same pipeline (A1/A2 below); (3) pricing the choice by **delivered AUC under a CPU frame deadline**, which none of the P2 papers do (they report GPU FPS). For a Q1 paper, P2 alone is a small add-on; "label policy explains most of it, stride adds X at iso-cost" is a real, publishable finding either way (positive or negative).

### A6. Assessment for our diagnosis and experiments

**Assessment.** The diagnosis (96% of the gap is *which people are detected*) fits the label-policy story. EXP-013 showed a labelled fine-tune is the big lever. P2 attacks a smaller, harder residual (the smallest people) at +67% FLOPs, which on a Pi (s ~ 2-4 already, prior lane) means more dropped frames. Its fair competitor is stock@800 (NCNN FP16 = 0.57x ORT-640 cost on the VM, +2.1 offline, EXP-012 `[local]`), not the 640 baseline. UBnormal is near chance for every arm, so it can only serve as a guard (F3 failed already).

**Experiment A0 (free gate, ~1 h CPU, no training): where do the people go after the fine-tune, and is the stride the limit?**
- Re-run the EXP-011 recall-by-height table (same matcher, frozen) on the EXP-013 fine-tuned weights (cached detections or a fresh pass at conf 0.01) at imgsz 640 and 800, **separately for anomalous frames only** and for all frames; also report recall at conf >= 0.01 vs >= 0.25.
- *Hypothesis H-A0:* after fine-tuning, pooled recall for reference people < 48 px (original px) is still <= 0.30 at 640, and those people are >= 20% of the reference persons in anomalous frames. *Pass (P2 is worth training):* both hold. *Fail:* the fine-tune already fixed the small-person recall or the missing people are not in anomalous frames, so P2 cannot move the AUC; write that up as the result and stop.
- Cost: hours of CPU. Risk: matcher bias for tiny boxes (already flagged in EXP-011); AlphaPose false positives among tiny references.

**Experiment A1 (one fine-tune + evaluation): label policy vs height (causal test of the COCO mechanism).**
- Train stock YOLOv8n-pose with the EXP-013 recipe three ways: (i) all persons (EXP-013 repro), (ii) only persons whose box height (original px) >= 83 (the COCO-like cutoff derived above) with the smaller people *masked out of the loss if possible, otherwise left unlabeled*, (iii) as (ii) but small people labelled as negatives (what COCO does). Same seed, frames, epochs.
- *H-A1:* labelling the small people is what matters: AUC(i) - AUC(ii) >= +3.0 on SHT L0 (paper variant) with the paired-bootstrap lower bound > 0, and recall < 64 px for (i) exceeds (ii) by >= 0.20. *Fail rule:* if AUC(i) - AUC(ii) < +1.0 the gain came from site adaptation of the larger people and the "COCO annotation policy" story is rejected.
- Cost: 2 extra trainings of EXP-013 size (hours each on MPS) + one evaluation each. Risk: ultralytics has no loss masking for unlabelled persons, so (ii) and (iii) are two different treatments (unlabelled vs explicit background); one seed only unless time allows.

**Experiment A2 (main P2 test, iso-cost): append-only P2 vs stock at 640 vs stock at 800.**
- Arms, all trained on the EXP-013 labels with the EXP-013 recipe, 3 seeds each: **S640** (stock fine-tune, EXP-013 repro), **S800** (stock fine-tune, trained and run at rect 480x800), **P2-640** (append-only P2, pre-flight output-equality gate passed, run at rect 384x640). Evaluate with the EXP-003/006 pipeline (c 0.25, frozen STG-NF), SHT L0 + L2, UBnormal L0 as guard; recall-by-height; coverage of anomalous and normal frames; VM ORT latency and NCNN FP16 latency (quiet host) for all three; delivered AUC under the 24-fps replay with P-fill at the measured trace.
- *H-A2a (accuracy):* P2-640 improves recall for people < 64 px by >= 0.15 absolute over S640 (pooled bins) AND SHT paper-variant AUC >= S640 + 1.5 with seed-averaged paired CI lower bound > 0, live variant not worse. *Guards (all must hold):* UB-L0 >= S640 - 0.5; rise in normal-frame coverage not larger than the rise in anomalous-frame coverage.
- *H-A2b (iso-cost):* P2-640 AUC >= S800 + 0.5 offline with CI lower bound > 0, OR delivered AUC at the measured Pi/VM trace >= S800 + 0.5; and P2-640 median latency <= 1.10x S800's on the same runtime.
- *Verdict rule:* **pass** = H-A2a and H-A2b; **partial** = H-A2a only (P2 helps but resolution is the cheaper way: report as "no gain over the 800-px knob"); **fail** = P2-640 <= S640 + 0.5 (then P2 is a null result, which is itself a finding given the pseudo-label gain already achieved at stride 8).
- Cost: 3 arms x 3 seeds of EXP-013-sized trainings (P2 ~1.7x slower per epoch `[D]` from FLOPs) + evaluation of 107 videos per condition per model; to limit cost run all conditions for seed 0 and SHT-L0 only for seeds 1-2. Latency timing needs a quiet host (owner rule).
- Risks: head and PAN re-initialisation (append-only mitigates, untested); more candidates per frame (top-K, NMS time, pose decode); AlphaPose noise for tiny people amplified by a head designed to use them (more false positives; the UB guard may fail like F3); sub-36-px people may simply carry no information at 856x480 (upsampling features does not create pixels, EXP-011: even 960 px recovers <1.3% of < 48-px people); site-specific gain (13 cameras); the NCNN export of the 4-level head is unverified.

## B. Temporal Shift Module (TSM) and relatives

### B1. Plain-language explanation of TSM

A 2D CNN looks at each video frame alone, so it knows nothing about what happened in the previous frame. 3D CNNs fix that but cost several times more compute. **TSM**'s trick: take the feature maps of all frames stacked in time, and for a *fraction* of the channels simply **slide them one step along the time axis** (a few channels are taken from the previous frame, a few from the next, the rest stay). A normal 2D convolution applied afterwards then mixes information from three frames, because the "current" frame now contains channels that belong to its neighbours. Moving data costs no multiplications and no parameters. Two design details matter: shift only a small part of the channels (1/8 forward + 1/8 backward = 1/4 total) to keep spatial ability and cut memory traffic, and put the shift inside the residual branch so the current frame's own features still flow through the shortcut. **Online TSM**: for live video, only shift from the *past*; at inference keep the shifted-out 1/8 of channels of each block in a small cache and paste it into the next frame's feature map, so each frame costs the same as a 2D network.

### B2. Sources (one line each)

- **TSM** (Lin, Gan, Han): shift part of the channels along time, "temporal modeling at zero computation and zero parameters"; partial shift 1/8 keeps overhead to ~3% CPU latency vs 13.7% when shifting all channels (ResNet-50, 8 frames, Xeon); residual shift beats in-place; best at 1/4 of channels shifted; online uni-directional version adds < 0.1 ms/frame on GPU (TVM), and online R-FCN on ImageNet-VID gives mAP 74.7 -> 76.3 overall and 51.4 -> 56.0 on fast-moving objects at the same latency (FGFA 75.9 at 2.5x); MobileNetV2 online TSM per-frame latency: Jetson Nano 47.8 ms CPU / 13.4 ms GPU, Jetson TX2 36.4 / 8.5 ms, Raspberry Pi 4B 69.6 ms, Galaxy Note8 34.5 ms, Pixel1 47.4 ms -> ICCV 2019 (venue from the task brief; the arXiv page text does not print it) https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08383 ; read: full (text and Tables 6-8, Figure 2 text).
- **Shift-GCN** (Cheng et al.): skeleton action recognition with spatial *and* temporal shift-graph operations plus pointwise convs; "more than 10x less computational complexity" (2.5 GFLOPs one-stream vs ST-GCN 16.2 GFLOPs counted for two persons; the temporal shift graph conv is about 9x lighter than a regular temporal graph conv); the *adaptive temporal shift* learns a fractional shift per channel (C extra parameters, 2NCT FLOPs) so receptive fields are not hand-set -> CVPR 2020 (oral) https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Cheng_Skeleton-Based_Action_Recognition_With_Shift_Graph_Convolutional_Network_CVPR_2020_paper.html ; supplementary https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/supplemental/Cheng_Skeleton-Based_Action_Recognition_CVPR_2020_supplemental.pdf ; read: full for abstract, method and FLOPs passages. It works at C >= tens of channels with pointwise convs.
- **ShiftGCN++**: further 6x fewer FLOPs, 2x practical speedup vs ShiftGCN-class, 4x practical speedup of ShiftGCN over prior GCNs, for low-power devices -> IEEE TIP 2021 https://doi.org/10.1109/tip.2021.3104182 ; read: abstract.
- **GSM** (Gate-Shift): learnable spatial gating decides which features go through a hard-coded time-shift (+32 pp on Something-Something-V1 over TSN at +0.48% params, +0.55% FLOPs) -> CVPR 2020 https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Sudhakaran_Gate-Shift_Networks_for_Video_Action_Recognition_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf ; read: partial (abstract + intro).
- **TEA**: motion excitation from feature-level temporal differences + multi-stage temporal aggregation with hierarchical sub-convs, "without additional parameters" -> CVPR 2020 https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Li_TEA_Temporal_Excitation_and_Aggregation_for_Action_Recognition_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf ; read: abstract.
- **TAM**: video-specific temporal kernels (local importance map + global aggregation weights) in a 2D CNN at small extra cost -> ICCV 2021 https://doi.org/10.1109/iccv48922.2021.01345 ; read: abstract.
- **TokShift**: shifts part of the [Class] token across frames in a video transformer; the paper reports that performance varies with the temporal sampling step and small steps are less effective -> arXiv 2108.02432 (preprint; later venue not confirmed) https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.02432 ; read: snippet. Closest hint that shift modules are sensitive to frame spacing.
- **Temporal-YOLOv8** (multi-frame input by packing 3 grayscale frames into the 3 colour channels; same cost as YOLOv8): in-house small-object data, mAP 0.465 -> 0.839 with temporal context *and* small-object augmentations together (not separable) -> Sensors 2024, 24(22):7387 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11598073/ ; read: abstract.
- **LSTFE-Net**: aligns short-term neighbour-frame features and selects long-term frames to help video small-object detection, +4.4 absolute AP on FL-Drones -> CVPR 2023 https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Xiao_LSTFE-Net_Long_Short-Term_Feature_Enhancement_Network_for_Video_Small_Object_Detection_CVPR_2023_paper.html ; read: abstract.
- **STG-NF** (our scorer): ~1K parameters, 0.95 GFLOPs, 189 FPS in the paper; with input segment length tau = 1 (no temporal context) AUC 75.1, with adjacency A = I (joints only connected to themselves in time) 83.9, full 85.9; "temporal behavior of each joint over time provides most of the information" -> ICCV 2023 https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Hirschorn_Normalizing_Flows_for_Human_Pose_Anomaly_Detection_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf ; read: full (excerpt of the ablation).
- **DA-Flow** (skeleton NF with a lightweight dual-attention module after the GCN; "fewest parameters" claim): temporal modelling added by attention/TCN, not shifts -> arXiv 2406.02976 (preprint); https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02976 ; read: abstract.
- **Shift-based normalizing flows or shift-based VAD:** none found in a web search (STG-NF, DA-Flow, DSNF (TIP 2025), MSG-Flow (arXiv 2603.26745) all use convolutions/attention/transformers); absence from web search only.
- **TSM under dropped frames / variable frame rate:** I found no paper that tests TSM with missing frames. TSM shifts by exactly one *index*, so with skipped frames the neighbour is a different time gap than during training `[I]`; TokShift's step sensitivity is the only indirect hint.

### B3. What the local code says about the scorer `[L]`

`edgelab/models/stgnf_flow.py` (same maths as STG-NF): 8 coupling steps, each a `GraphSubnet` = `st_gcn(in=1, out=2, kernel=(n_frames//2+1, A.size(0)))`. At 24 frames the temporal kernel is **13 taps**, and the temporal conv is `Conv2d(2, 2, (13,1))` = 54 weights per step; 8 steps = 432 weights, about 70% of the 616 parameters `[D]` (the rest: the joint-mixing 1x1 conv, 4 BatchNorm and ActNorm). EXP-019: standard flow 0.94 ms/window (1 thread, batch 1, Mac); GAU variant 5,808 parameters, 1.93 ms, no AUC gain (-0.78, G1 fail) with much lower training loss and no better test AUC (suggests over-fitting, not tested).

### B4. Assessment, idea by idea

**(1) Zero-parameter temporal mixing inside STG-NF's coupling subnet. Verdict: do not pursue as an accuracy idea; at best an appendix non-inferiority test.**
- TSM needs many channels (it shifts 1/8 + 1/8 of C); the coupling input is **one** channel and the output **two**. Shifting a fraction of one channel is meaningless; shifting whole channels with fixed offsets turns the 13-tap learned filter into a fixed sparse filter with *less* expressiveness, plus zero saved compute worth having (0.94 ms/window).
- The flow already has a full-window temporal receptive field (13 taps over 24 frames, repeated 8 times). STG-NF's own ablation shows temporal modelling is the information source (75.1 -> 83.9 -> 85.9), but the GAU result shows that *adding* temporal capacity does not help; the scorer is not temporal-capacity-limited, and our retrained standard scorer is within 0.9 AUC of the released one.
- Shift-GCN is the only skeleton shift precedent and it replaces temporal convs with shift + pointwise conv at C = 64-256 channels in a classifier, for FLOPs reasons (10x). Our scorer has no FLOPs problem. Expected AUC effect: <= 0, at best non-inferior with fewer parameters.

**(2) Online TSM in the detector for temporal consistency. Verdict: the only TSM idea with possible payoff; conditional on a free diagnostic; engineering-heavy.**
- Evidence for: online TSM gives +1.6 mAP and +4.6 on fast-moving objects on ImageNet-VID at the same latency (partial shift, no flow); Temporal-YOLOv8 and LSTFE-Net show multi-frame context helps small objects in video (but with other changes bundled, and on aerial/in-house data, not CCTV pedestrians).
- Evidence against / limits for us: (a) EXP-011/EXP-003 H3: the people we miss are mostly *never* detected, even at low thresholds (the cache keeps conf 0.01); temporal fusion helps when evidence is weak but present in some frames, not when it is absent in all frames `[I]`. (b) TSM's ImageNet-VID objects are not tiny; I found no TSM result on tiny-person detection. (c) The shift is not motion-compensated; a person moving 2-10 px between processed frames is ~1 cell at stride 8, which matters for a 36-px person `[I]`. (d) Ultralytics trains on single images; this needs a clip dataloader and a custom training loop; the stateful cache must be exported through ORT and NCNN as extra inputs/outputs per shifted block (feasible, not trivial `[I]`).
- **Dropped frames (our regime, s = 2-4 on a Pi):** with a deadline the previous *processed* frame is 2-6 frames old, not 1. A TSM trained on consecutive frames will see a different gap at test time (TokShift hint). Mitigation: train with random strides in {1, 3, 6}, as EXP-010 does for fills. This is the part of the idea I would call new in this setting (no source found).
- Edge cost: shift is data movement only; the cache is 1/8 of the channels of each shifted residual block; for a YOLOv8n-scale backbone that is kilobytes-to-about-a-megabyte of activations per stream `[D, order of magnitude only]`; TSM paper's measured overhead at 1/8 is ~3% CPU latency (ResNet-50, Xeon, partial shift), < 0.1 ms on GPU for the online version. On an A76 with NCNN the cost is **not measured by anyone I found**.

**(3) Shift with missing frames at the scorer. Verdict: not a TSM problem.** The scorer's window already tolerates <= 2 missing frames, and EXP-018 showed FILL-HOLD beats SKIP by +4.7 and a learned GRU filler adds +0.5. Replacing "filled" frames by a shifted neighbour is exactly FILL-HOLD. No new evidence supports a shift-specific benefit.

**(4) Edge cost summary.** Scorer: negligible whether shift or conv. Detector: +~3% (TSM paper, partial shift) plus cache I/O; it is *cheaper than P2* (+67% FLOPs) and cheaper than 800 px (+56%), which is the point if it works at all.

### B5. Verdict

Of the TSM ideas only **(2) online TSM in the detector, trained with random frame strides**, deserves an experiment, and only after the free diagnostic below says missed small people are intermittent. Everything about STG-NF (1, 3) is predicted null or redundant. Prior that (2) passes: low-to-moderate (my guess 15-25%, `[I]`); it ranks below A0/A1/A2 in expected value per day of work.

### B6. Experiments

**Experiment B0 (free gate, same machinery as A0): how much of the miss is intermittent?**
- Using cached detections (conf >= 0.01) and the released reference poses, for every reference person-frame that is **missed** at conf 0.25 (EXP-011 matcher), classify it by height bin as: (a) *same-frame weak*: a matching box exists at conf in [0.01, 0.25); (b) *intermittent*: the same person (via reference track ID if the released poses carry one, otherwise IoU linking) is detected at conf >= 0.25 in at least one of the frames t +/- 5; (c) *never seen* within +/- 5 frames and no weak box.
- *H-B0:* in the 48-96 px bins, (a) + (b) >= 40% of missed person-frames on SHT L0 at 640. *Pass:* a temporal method has headroom (go to B1). *Fail:* temporal consistency cannot recover most misses; write "missing small people are absent, not flickering" and stop; the lever is training data and resolution (A1/A2).
- Cost: hours of CPU, no training. Risk: reference track IDs and tiny-person false positives in the reference; IoU linking errors.

**Experiment B1 (conditional on B0): online TSM backbone with stride-randomised fine-tune.**
- Insert uni-directional residual partial shift (1/8 of channels from the previous processed frame) into the C2f bottleneck residual branches of YOLOv8n-pose at P3-P5; weights are unchanged (zero parameters), so start from the EXP-013 fine-tuned model. Fine-tune on SHT train clips (3-4 frames per sample, strides drawn from {1, 3, 6}) with the EXP-013 pseudo-labels, **control = identical clip training with the shift disabled** (so the clip dataloader and extra epochs are not credited to TSM). Inference keeps a per-stream cache; when a frame is dropped the cache is simply not updated.
- *H-B1:* at s = 1 and at s = 3 (60% dropped, with P-fill), SHT paper-variant AUC >= control + 1.0 with paired CI lower bound > 0 in both, recall for 48-96 px people >= control + 0.05, tracks per reference person (fragmentation) <= control - 10% relative; *guard:* UB-L0 >= control - 0.5; latency (VM ORT, cache I/O included) <= 1.05x control. *Fail rule:* if the s = 3 gain is < +0.3 while s = 1 is positive, report "temporal shift works only on consecutive frames, not under deadlines".
- Cost: 3-5 days engineering (clip dataloader, training loop, stateful export), then one EXP-013-size training x 2 arms x 3 seeds + evaluation. Risk: stale/misaligned cache for tiny moving people; BN statistics; NCNN stateful graph export; effect swamped by video-bootstrap noise (+/- 1.4 AUC seen in EXP-019); needs a Pi to price the cache copy.

**Experiment B2 (optional, appendix only, ~3 h CPU): shift taps in the scorer as a compression test.**
- Replace the 13-tap temporal conv of each coupling subnet by fixed shifts of the single input channel at offsets {-6, -3, -1, +1, +3, +6} stacked as channels followed by the existing 1x1 mixing (no new temporal weights); same recipe, 3 seeds, EXP-019 protocol.
- *H-B2 (non-inferiority, not superiority):* mean AUC >= standard - 0.5 on released poses, edge poses and top-down poses, with <= 0.6x parameters of the standard model. *Pass:* all three. Expected outcome: parameters shrink by ~430 and latency by < 0.5 ms; scientific value is a second negative-or-neutral scorer result ("temporal mixing form is not the bottleneck") alongside GAU. Not worth more than that.
- Cost: 3 trainings x ~50 min (EXP-019 standard models took 48-57 min on CPU). Risk: fixed taps may under-fit; difference inside the +/-1.4 AUC bootstrap noise, so only non-inferiority can be claimed.
